Our storyFrom sleeping on the couch to homes around the world

Where it startedFrom sleeping on the couch behind a beach blanket and subletting my own bedroom…

  1. We met as housemates

    In our early twenties we both left home, Belgium and Brazil, for Barcelona. In 2017 Farah rented a room from Daniel. He slept in a walk-in closet so he could rent out the bigger rooms in his shared apartment. That is how we met (very on brand, haha).

  2. €67.50 left in the bank

    When Farah got her own rental apartment, she ended up with only €67.50 in her account. Broke, she furnished it with things she found on the street, sublet her bedroom on Airbnb and slept on the couch behind a beach blanket. It wasn't glamorous, but it worked.

  3. Wait a second… let's go all in

    That is when we realized this could make real money. At first we did everything ourselves, from cleaning to YouTube tutorials on fixing a toilet. When the Airbnb rules got stricter, we adapted to long-term stays of 31+ nights instead of shutting down, and it worked.

  4. Things really took off

    Once things started to work, we got our dream rooftop apartment in Barcelona and scaled to multiple apartments with co-living. Then we got our dream villa in Bali. One thing led to another, and we managed to create a six-figure hosting business. The wildest part? We still don't own a single one of our properties.

  5. Today: mostly passive income

    Payout notifications come in while we are out enjoying life. Bookings keep rolling in and the 5-star reviews stay consistent, because our team runs the daily operations and our managers reply to guest messages.

Was it always easy? Definitely not. Is it possible? 100%.

Still unsure?You might be thinking

  • “Is short-term renting even legal where I live?”

    It depends on the local rules

    Rules differ from city to city, so you always have to research your local regulations. Even in Barcelona, one of the strictest cities in the world, we operate legally. There are opportunities everywhere, and lesson 2 shows you how to check the rules for your city.

  • “I don't own any property.”

    You don't need to

    With the owner's written agreement you can sublet. It is called rental arbitrage, and it is how we run every apartment we have.

  • “I don't have a big budget.”

    You can start small

    Co-hosting needs no budget at all. With less than $10k you can secure a rental, furnish it and launch.

  • “What if guests damage the place?”

    You are covered

    It was one of our biggest fears too. Airbnb covers hosts for up to $3 million in damage, and lesson 7 shows you how to guest-proof the space.

AI prompt: discover the local rental rules in your area
Can you help me find the current rules for renting out a property in ? I want to understand the rules for short-term rentals (under 1 month), mid-term rentals (1 to 6 months) and long-term rentals, and for subletting a property I rent (rental arbitrage). For each type of rental, I want to know: - If it is legal, and under which conditions - Whether I need a license, permit or registration - Any minimum or maximum stay rules, or limits on the number of nights per year - Tax obligations (income tax, tourist or occupancy tax) - Zoning restrictions, primary residence rules or building/HOA rules - What a landlord needs to agree to if I want to sublet - Whether I can do this as a private individual, or need to register as self-employed or a company Please include any helpful links or resources to official government sites if available. At the end, add a short summary I can copy into my notes, in this format: - Short-term rentals (under 1 month): allowed / not allowed / with conditions - Mid-term rentals (1–6 months): allowed / not allowed / with conditions - Long-term rentals: allowed / not allowed / with conditions - Subletting (rental arbitrage): allowed / not allowed / with conditions - License or permit needed: yes / no (and which one) - Minimum stay: ... - Taxes: ... - Other important rules: ...
