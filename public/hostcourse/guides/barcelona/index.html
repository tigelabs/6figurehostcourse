BarcelonaHost Guide

+ mid-term rental toolkit

How to run a legal, profitable rental business in Barcelona with mid-term stays, and copy the model to any city in Europe.

by Farah & Daniel

Welcome to Barcelona

Barcelona is where it all started for us. A suitcase, a shared flat, a couch with a beach blanket hanging from the window. Today it is where we run most of our business, in one of the most regulated rental markets in the world.

This guide is the add-on to our 6-Figure Host Course. The course shows you how to launch, run and scale a hosting business anywhere. This one shows you how to do it in a city that is actively trying to stop short-term rentals, and still build something profitable, legal and calm.

Section 1

What you will learn

In Part 1, we help you understand Barcelona: the numbers, the three ways to rent, what really happens with tourist licences, and the neighbourhoods.

In Part 2, we explain the legal model we use: mid-term rentals of 32 days or more, renting by rooms under the new Catalan rules, the platforms, and whether to do it as an individual or through a company, with the taxes for each.

In Part 3, we run and scale it: day-to-day operations, the real numbers of an apartment, and how to take this model to other European cities.

“If you can make it work in Barcelona, you can make it work almost anywhere in Europe.”
Section 2

The honest truth before you start

Barcelona is one of the best rental markets in Europe. It is also one of the strictest. Before you invest a single euro, you need to know this:

!

No new tourist licences

The only way to rent legally for less than 32 days is to own a flat that already has a licence, and all of those licences are set to end in November 2028.

✓

Mid-term is the real opportunity

Stays of 32 days or more for work, study or other temporary needs are allowed, and demand is huge.

!

Mid-term is regulated too

Since January 2026, Catalan law caps their rent in stressed areas, requires you to document the reason for the stay and limits how much you can charge for rooms.

!

Taxes matter as much as rent

Choosing between renting as an individual or through a company can change your net income by thousands of euros a year.

€600,000The maximum fine for operating an illegal tourist flat in Barcelona.

Does that mean you should not invest? No. It means you need to understand the rules better than everyone else. Every time the rules get stricter, the people who do it right get less competition.

“In Barcelona, the legal model is not the safe option. It is the only option.”

A city everyone wants to live in

Barcelona is not only a holiday destination. It is a city people move to: to study, to work, to start a company, to try a new life by the sea. That constant flow of newcomers is the engine of the mid-term rental model.

Look at what those numbers tell you. Tourist demand is enormous, but the supply of legal tourist flats is fixed and shrinking. Taxes on short stays are going up every year. Part 1 · Lesson 3

Tourist licences (HUT) and the 2028 deadline

Is buying a flat with a licence still worth it?

2 sections · about 5 minLesson03
Section 1

What happens with tourist licences

A tourist licence in Barcelona is called HUT (habitatge d’ús turístic). Without one, you cannot legally rent a flat to tourists for 31 nights or less. Here is the situation:

1

No new licences

Barcelona stopped granting new tourist-flat licences years ago. The number is frozen at around 10,101.

2

Licences belong to the property

The only way to get one is to buy an apartment that already has it. These flats sell at a premium, often quoted at €60,000–120,000 above similar flats.

3

They end in November 2028

In June 2024, Mayor Jaume Collboni announced that the city will not renew any of them, based on Catalan decree-law 3/2023, which lets municipalities in stressed areas limit or end tourist-flat permits.

4

The courts backed it

In March 2025, Spain’s Constitutional Court upheld the Catalan framework.

5

Fines are huge

Fines for illegal tourist flats can reach €600,000.

Section 2

Is a licence still worth it?

Short-term rentals are very profitable in Barcelona, that has not changed. But you would be paying a premium for a licence that, unless the rules change, has around two years left. Run the numbers as if the licence ends in 2028: how much extra profit will you make until then, and does it pay back the premium you pay today?

Pro tip: when you buy a flat with a HUT, check that the licence is valid, registered to that exact address and has no pending sanctions. And plan from day one what the apartment becomes after 2028.

3 sections · about 5 minLesson04
Section 1

The tourist hotspots

Barcelona is a city of neighbourhoods, and each one attracts a different guest. These are the areas everyone knows. Demand is huge, but so is competition and pressure from the city.

Ciutat Vella

Gòtic, El Born, El Raval, Barceloneta

The historic centre and the beach. Unbeatable location for tourists, but also where the city is most aggressive against tourist rentals. Old buildings, often without lifts, noise at night. For mid-term, El Born and Gòtic attract students and young professionals who want to live the city; Raval is cheaper and more mixed.

Eixample

Dreta and Esquerra

Wide streets, modernist buildings, big flats with high ceilings. Central, safe and well connected. A favourite for professionals, families and longer stays. Large apartments here work very well for room rentals.

Gràcia

A village inside the city

Small squares, independent shops, a strong local identity. Very popular with students, creatives and international young professionals. Flats are smaller, demand is constant.

Sant Antoni & Poble-sec

Trendy and foodie

Close to the centre and Montjuïc. Great for young professionals and digital nomads. Prices have risen fast in recent years.

Outside the postcard areas you often find better prices, bigger flats, less competition and guests who stay longer.

Poblenou

The old industrial district turned tech hub (22@). Startups, offices, the beach and lofts. Perfect for professionals on assignment and remote workers.

Sants

Next to the main train station, very well connected, more local and affordable. Great value for students and newcomers.

Les Corts

Residential and calm, close to the university area of Diagonal and big business schools. Strong demand from students.

Part 2 · Lesson 5

Seasonal rentals done right

Stays of 32 days or more, under the 2026 Catalan law.

3 sections · about 5 minLesson05
Section 1

What the 2026 Catalan law requires

For years, seasonal rentals were the “free zone” of the Spanish rental market. That changed in Catalonia with Law 11/2025 of 29 December on housing and urban planning, in force since the start of January 2026. Here is what it means for you:

1

A real temporary reason

A seasonal rental is one made for professional, work, study or medical reasons, for provisional situations (waiting for a new home, returning to your usual residence) or similar temporary housing needs.

2

Write it in the contract

The contract must state the temporary reason and the tenant’s permanent address.

3

Keep the proof

Documents that prove the reason (work contract, enrolment letter, medical appointment…) are deposited with the tenant’s deposit (fianza) at the official Catalan deposit register (INCASÒL). If no temporary use is recorded there, the law presumes the rental is a permanent home.

4

Rent caps apply

In stressed residential market areas, which today cover almost all of Catalonia, seasonal rentals are subject to the same rent limits as permanent housing.

5

Extensions are possible

As long as the temporary reason continues and is documented in writing. A new contract with the same tenant for the same flat is treated as a permanent rental unless you can prove the temporary need still exists.

Holiday or leisure rentals are outside this law, but they must state it in the contract, and in Barcelona short stays for leisure need a tourist licence. Do not try to disguise a tourist stay as a seasonal one. It is exactly what inspectors look for.

Section 2

Your guests and their documents

The good news: the typical mid-term guest in Barcelona already has a valid temporary reason. You just need to ask for it and keep it organised.

GuestTemporary reasonDocument to keep
Student / ErasmusStudyEnrolment or acceptance letter with dates
InternProfessional trainingInternship agreement
Worker on assignmentWorkWork contract, letter from employer or assignment letter
NewcomerTemporary need while finding a permanent homeNew job contract in Barcelona and proof of home search, or similar
Patient / companionMedical treatmentAppointment or treatment letter
Renovating their homeProvisional situationRenovation permit or works contract
Our process

We ask for the document before confirming the booking, we write the reason and the guest’s permanent address in the contract, and we deposit both with the fianza. It takes ten minutes per guest, and it is what keeps the whole business legal.

Pro tip: when you rent through Airbnb for 32+ nights, still sign your own seasonal contract with the guest. The platform booking alone does not include the clauses and documents the law asks for.

Part 2 · Lesson 6

Renting by rooms and the new rent cap

The sum-of-rooms rule, and adding real services the right way.

3 sections · about 5 minLesson06
Section 1

The sum-of-rooms rule

Renting by rooms is one of the most profitable ways to use a large apartment in Barcelona. A four-bedroom flat rented room by room has usually earned much more than the same flat rented as a whole. The regulators noticed.

The same Law 11/2025 added a specific rule for room rentals (article 66 ter of the Catalan Housing Law). In a stressed residential area, when a dwelling is rented by rooms for housing purposes, the sum of all the room rents cannot exceed the maximum rent that would apply to the whole dwelling.

An example

Imagine the maximum legal rent for your whole flat is €1,800 per month. You have four rooms. Under the new rule, the four room rents together cannot go above €1,800, so an average of €450 per room. If you charged €900 per room (€3,600), you would be €1,800 over the cap.

Charging above the cap is a serious infringement when the excess is up to 30%, and a very serious infringement when it is more than 30%, under Catalan decree-law 1/2025.

Section 2

What this means in practice

1

Know your cap first

Before you buy or rent a flat. It depends on the official reference index for that flat (size, location, features). Your lawyer or the Catalan rent index tool can calculate it.

2

Capped, not banned

Room rentals still work. The margins come from services, efficiency and the right flat, not from inflating rent.

3

Follow the news

Some measures are being challenged in court and the details may change. Follow the news and your lawyer’s advice.

Section 3

Adding real services the right way

Here is how we think about it. A mid-term room in our apartments is not just four walls. Our guests get a furnished room, internet, utilities, weekly cleaning of the shared areas, maintenance, a responsive manager and help settling into the city. Those are real services with real costs.

Under the new rules, the rent is capped. Services that are genuinely provided, clearly described and priced transparently can be invoiced separately, for example:

Cleaning feeWeekly cleaning of common areas, deep clean at check-out.
Management feeGuest support, coordination, maintenance requests, administration.
Optional extrasLinen and towel service, extra room cleaning, airport pick-up.
The fine print (read this twice)
  • The services must be real and actually delivered. A “fee” with nothing behind it is just disguised rent.
  • Catalan law warns that mandatory add-ons that, in practice, push the price above the cap can be treated as a breach. In stressed areas, the LAU also limits passing new expenses to the tenant that were not in the previous contract.
  • Services can have VAT consequences (lesson 8).
Our approach
  • We keep the rent within the cap.
  • We price services at what they really cost us plus a fair margin.
  • Each service and its price is written in the contract, separate from the rent. Where possible, extras are optional.
  • Have your lawyer review your services menu before you use it.
“Charge for value you deliver, never for a loophole.”

Before you sign a flat, run its full monthly numbers in lesson 10. If the numbers only work above the cap, it is not a deal.

Part 2 · Lesson 7

Platforms, registration and enforcement

What changed in 2025 and 2026.

1 sections · about 5 minLesson07
Section 1

Platforms and registration in 2026

Most of our mid-term guests find us on platforms: Airbnb (with monthly stays), and specialist sites for mid-term rentals and students. Here is what changed recently:

1

The national registry was annulled

From July 2025, Spain required a single registration number (NRUA) for tourist and seasonal listings. In May 2026, the Supreme Court annulled it, ruling that the State overstepped regional powers. The digital data-sharing system for platforms remains.

2

Regional rules still apply

In Catalonia, tourist flats need their HUT registration, and seasonal rentals must follow Law 11/2025 (contract, documents, deposit).

3

EU rules arrived

The EU Short-Term Rental Regulation (2024/1028) applies from 20 May 2026. Platforms must share activity data with authorities, display registration numbers where required, and remove listings when authorities order it.

4

Enforcement is real

Spanish authorities fined Airbnb €64 million and Barcelona has a long record of closing illegal flats.

What this means for you

Your listings and your contracts must tell the same story. If your listing says “monthly stays for professionals and students”, your contracts, documents and deposits must prove it.

Pro tip: set your minimum stay on every platform to 32 nights. One accidental 10-night booking is enough to turn a legal seasonal rental into an illegal tourist one.

Part 2 · Lesson 8

Individual or company? (taxes explained)

IRPF, corporate tax, VAT and dividends.

5 sections · about 5 minLesson08
Section 1

Starting as an individual

If you start with one apartment or a few rooms, renting as an individual (persona física) is usually simplest. You declare the income in your annual personal income tax return (IRPF).

Progressive tax

Net rental income (rent minus deductible costs) is added to your other income. For 2026, the state brackets go from 9.5% to 24.5%, plus the regional part. In Catalonia the top combined marginal rate reaches 50%.

Deductible costs

Mortgage interest, community fees, IBI (property tax), insurance, repairs, utilities you pay, management and cleaning services, and depreciation of the building and furniture.

No rental reduction

The 50% reduction is for renting a habitual home. Seasonal rentals do not qualify. Rooms rented as the tenant’s habitual residence can qualify, which is a different model.

If you are not a Spanish tax resident, different rules (non-resident income tax) apply. Talk to an advisor.

This works well while the numbers are small. But because IRPF is progressive, every extra apartment you add is taxed at a higher marginal rate. With two or three properties, the tax bill can grow faster than the business.

Section 2

When a company makes sense

Once you have two or three properties, or you plan to keep growing, it often makes sense to operate through a limited company (Sociedad Limitada, SL). That is what we recommend to anyone who wants to build this as a real business, fully legal and organised.

Company typeCorporate tax rate 2026
General rate25%
Micro-companies19% on the first €50,000 of profit, 21% on the rest (turnover under €1M)
Newly created15% (first years with profit)

Important: a company that only owns and rents property, without a real economic activity, can be considered an asset-holding company and lose the reduced rates. For rental activity, tax rules generally look for at least one employee working full time in the business. Ask your advisor how to structure it.

✓ Flat corporate rate instead of up to 50%✓ Reinvested profit stays in the company✓ Professional structure: contracts, insurance, financing, partners✓ Personal tax only when you take money out
Section 3

VAT: when it applies

VAT (IVA) is the part most hosts get wrong, so let’s make it simple.

1

Plain rental is exempt

If you only rent the space (even furnished, with cleaning at check-in and check-out), you do not charge VAT. The downside: you also cannot recover the VAT you pay on furniture, renovations or services.

2

Hotel-type services add VAT

Services typical of the hotel industry during the stay, such as periodic cleaning of the guest’s space or regular changes of bedding and towels, make it accommodation subject to VAT (reduced 10% rate). Optional extras sold separately are taxed as independent services.

3

Charge VAT, deduct VAT

A company that charges VAT on its services can deduct the VAT it pays on furniture, renovations, cleaning, supplies and professional fees, and in some cases get it refunded. On a full renovation and furnishing, that can be thousands of euros.

Decide before you renovate

Which option is better depends on your guests (individuals cannot deduct VAT, companies can), your investment plan and your service model. The VAT you can recover depends on how you set up the activity from the start. VAT is declared quarterly (form 303) and annually (form 390). Keep every invoice in the company’s name.

Section 4

Dividends and a worked example

When you take profits out of your company as dividends, they are taxed in your personal savings income. In 2026 the rates are 19% up to €6,000, 21% up to €50,000, 23% up to €200,000, 27% up to €300,000 and 30% above. The company withholds 19% when it pays the dividend.

Part 3 · Lesson 9

How we run a mid-term apartment day to day

Rooms, contracts, cleaning and house rules.

3 sections · about 5 minLesson09
Section 1

Setting up a mid-term apartment

A mid-term guest is not a tourist. They will work, study, cook, receive packages and live their life in your apartment for weeks or months. Design for living, not for a weekend.

In every room
  • A lock on every bedroom door
  • A real desk and chair
  • A double bed with a quality mattress and blackout curtains
  • Storage: wardrobe, drawers, and a labelled shelf in the kitchen and fridge for each guest
  • Fast Wi-Fi that reaches every room
In the flat
  • A fully equipped kitchen: guests cook every day
  • Washing machine and drying rack
  • Heating and air conditioning: Barcelona summers are hot, winters are damp
  • Water filter in the kitchen and shower
  • Smart lock or key box for flexible arrivals

The course has the full furnishing list, the label system and the decluttering process for shared spaces. Use them here: shared apartments live or die by how clear and organised the common areas are.

Think “home for a few months”, not “hotel for a few nights”. That is what gets you long stays and great reviews.
Section 2

Contracts, cleaning and house rules

This is the routine that keeps our apartments full, legal and calm:

1

Screen before you confirm

We ask why they are coming, for how long, and for the document that proves it. If it does not fit a seasonal reason, we do not accept the booking.

2

Sign the seasonal contract

With the reason, dates, the guest’s permanent address, rent within the cap and the services listed separately.

3

File the deposit

Collect and file the deposit at the official register with the documents.

4

Clean the shared areas weekly

Kitchen, bathrooms, living room, hallway. It is the service our guests value most, and it keeps the flat in perfect condition for the next guest.

5

Clear house rules

Quiet hours, guests and visitors, cleaning duties, shared fridge, recycling. Printed in the flat and attached to the contract.

6

One contact person

Who answers fast and handles maintenance.

7

Check-out inspection

With photos, a deep clean and a room reset within 24 hours.

Pro tip: the best marketing for mid-term rentals is your current guests. Students and interns recommend you to their classmates and colleagues. Offer a small referral thank-you.

Mid-term guests often need to register at the town hall (empadronamiento) for paperwork. Decide your policy with your lawyer and explain it before they book.

Section 3

Bonus: guest documents checklist

For every guest, before they move in. Keep everything in one folder per guest (Notion or Google Drive): if an inspector or tax office ever asks, you can show the full file in minutes.

Section 1

How to read the numbers

Part 3 · Lesson 11

Take the model to other European cities

Barcelona is your training ground.

2 sections · about 5 minLesson11
Section 1

Barcelona is your training ground

Here is the best part: once you understand this model in Barcelona, you understand it in most of Europe. Big European cities are all fighting the same battle: housing shortages, overtourism and pressure from residents. The tools change name, but they look very similar:

Night caps

Amsterdam, for example, allows only 30 nights per year for primary residences.

Licences and registration

And principal-residence rules in cities like Paris, Berlin and Lisbon.

Platform data sharing

Since 20 May 2026, the EU Short-Term Rental Regulation makes platforms share data with authorities and remove listings when ordered.

Rent controls

Spreading from long-term to seasonal rentals, as in Catalonia.

And almost everywhere, the same opportunity: people who move to a city for work, study or a new life need a furnished place for a few months. That demand does not depend on tourism, and in most countries these rentals are treated as housing, not as tourist accommodation.

Rules are generally softer in smaller cities and regions with lower population pressure. Coastal towns, university cities and secondary cities often have fewer restrictions, lower purchase prices and less competition, while still having universities, hospitals and companies that bring mid-term guests.

“Learn the model in the hardest city. Then take it wherever the rules are kinder.”
