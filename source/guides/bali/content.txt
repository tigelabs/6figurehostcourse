<h2>Welcome to Bali</h2>
<p>Imagine waking up to the sound of the jungle, walking barefoot to your own pool and knowing that the villa around you pays for itself, and then some. Bali can give you that. <strong>But Bali also eats unprepared investors for breakfast.</strong></p>
<p>This guide is the add-on to our 6-Figure Host Course. The course teaches you how to launch, run and scale a hosting business anywhere. This one goes deep on a single island: <strong>the rules, the land, the paperwork, the builders, the rain, the water and the trash.</strong> Everything we wish someone had told us before we signed our first lease in Bali.</p>
</section>

<div class="pboxes">

<div class="pbox"><p><strong class="blue">In Part 2, we set you up legally:</strong> your PT PMA company, your investor visa and the licences you need to rent short-term without risking a closure notice.</p></div>

<blockquote class="pullquote">“In Bali, the right partner saves you more money than any negotiation.”</blockquote>
::part The honest truth before you start
<p>Bali is one of the most rewarding places in the world to run a holiday rental. It is also one of the easiest places to lose money if you treat it like a holiday. Here is what nobody tells you in the villa tours:</p>
<div class="mk-grid">
<div class="mk"><span class="mk-x">!</span><h4>[[ic:key]]You cannot own land</h4><p>As a foreigner, you can lease it, use it, or hold it through a company, and each option has rules.</p></div>
<div class="mk"><span class="mk-x">!</span><h4>[[ic:alert]]The colour of the land matters most</h4><p>More than the view. Build on the wrong zone and your villa can be sealed, fined or, in the worst cases, demolished.</p></div>
<div class="mk"><span class="mk-x">!</span><h4>[[ic:wrench]]Many villas are built to be sold</h4><p>Not to last. Cheap materials and badly filled rice fields show up one or two rainy seasons later.</p></div>
<div class="mk"><span class="mk-x">!</span><h4>[[ic:calendar]]Bali is getting stricter</h4><p>Unlicensed rentals are being delisted from booking platforms and foreigners working on the wrong visa are deported.</p></div>
<div class="mk"><span class="mk-x">✓</span><h4>[[ic:sparkle]]The island runs on relationships</h4><p>Your banjar (village council), your staff and your neighbours matter as much as your paperwork.</p></div>
</div>
<p>Does that mean you should not invest? No. It means the bar is higher, and that is exactly your opportunity. <strong>When the rules get tighter, the hosts who did it right get less competition, better rankings and higher prices.</strong> Our goal with this guide is simple: help you be one of them.</p>
<blockquote class="pullquote">“In Bali, the cheapest deal is usually the most expensive one.”</blockquote>

::part The demand: tourists keep coming
<p>Before you look at a single villa, look at the market. Numbers do not lie, and in Bali they tell a clear story: <strong>demand is still growing.</strong></p>
<div class="vk-two">
<div class="vk-stat"><b>6.95M</b><span>direct foreign tourist arrivals in Bali in 2025 (BPS Bali).</span></div>
<div class="vk-stat"><b>+9.7%</b><span>growth in foreign arrivals vs 2024.</span></div>
<div class="vk-stat"><b>23%</b><span>of foreign visitors came from Australia, the largest source market.</span></div>
<div class="vk-stat"><b>IDR 150K</b><span>tourist levy per foreign visitor, in place since February 2024.</span></div>

<p>On top of foreign visitors, Bali receives millions of domestic Indonesian tourists every year, especially during Indonesian holidays.</p>
<div class="vk-tip"><b>Demand is seasonal.</b> The busiest months are usually the European and Australian summer holidays (July and August) and Christmas to New Year. The rainy season, roughly November to March, is quieter outside the holidays, so your pricing strategy has to move with it.</div>

<p>Bali has close to 60,000 classified hotel rooms (Colliers, 2023), and that is before you count villas, guesthouses and homestays. AirROI tracked around 34,500 active Airbnb listings in Bali in the 12 months to September 2026. Here is what the average listing achieved:</p>

::lesson ch3 | 03 | Who can own what | Part 1 · Lesson 3 | Who can own what | (freehold vs leasehold) | Hak Milik, Hak Sewa, Hak Pakai and HGB explained.
::part Freehold is for Indonesians only
<p>This is the first rule of Bali real estate, and the one most people get wrong: <strong>a foreigner cannot own freehold land in Indonesia.</strong> Freehold, called Hak Milik, is reserved for Indonesian citizens. Not for foreigners, and not for foreign-owned companies (PT PMA). If someone offers you “freehold”, ask immediately: in whose name?</p>
<div class="gold"><span class="gold-k">Never use a nominee</span><p>The answer is often a nominee: an Indonesian person who holds the title on paper for you, with a side agreement saying the property is really yours. <strong>Nominee arrangements give you no legal protection.</strong> If the nominee passes away, divorces, gets into debt or simply changes their mind, the land is legally theirs.</p></div>
<p>So what can you do as a foreigner? You have four legal routes:</p>
<div class="cmp-wrap"><table class="cmp"><thead><tr><th>Title</th><th>What it is</th><th>Duration</th><th>Best for</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><th>Hak Sewa (leasehold)</th><td>You rent the land (and villa) from the owner for a fixed term</td><td>Usually 25–30 years, extension agreed in the contract</td><td><strong>Most villa investors</strong></td></tr>
<tr><th>Hak Pakai (right to use)</th><td>Title for foreigners with a KITAS/KITAP to use a residential property</td><td>30 + 20 + 30 years (max 80)</td><td>Your own home, not rentals</td></tr>
<tr><th>HGB (right to build)</th><td>Right to build and own buildings, held by a company like a PT PMA</td><td>30 + 20 + 30 years (max 80)</td><td>Larger and commercial projects</td></tr>
<tr><th>Hak Milik (freehold)</th><td>Full ownership</td><td>Forever</td><td>Indonesian citizens only</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>
::part Leasehold: how it really works
<p>Leasehold (Hak Sewa) is how most foreigners invest in Bali, us included. You lease the land, and usually the villa on it, from the Indonesian owner for a fixed number of years. A few things that surprise people:</p>
<div class="vk-two">
<div class="vk-card"><h4>[[ic:box]]You pay upfront</h4><p>A 25-year lease is usually paid in full on signing, not monthly.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>[[ic:refresh]]It goes back to the owner</h4><p>At the end of the lease, the land and everything built on it go back to the owner, unless you extend. <strong>That is why the extension clause is the most important part of your contract.</strong></p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>[[ic:key]]A passport is enough to sign</h4><p>You do not need a KITAS to sign a lease. But to rent it out commercially, you will need a company and licences (Part 2).</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>[[ic:alert]]Always a notarial deed</h4><p>The lease is signed before a notary as a notarial deed (Akta Sewa Menyewa). Never sign a private agreement only.</p></div>
</div>
<p>Think of leasehold like this: <strong>you are buying years of income.</strong> A 25-year lease on a villa that earns well can be an excellent investment, but its value goes down every year that passes. So when you compare villas, always compare <strong>price per remaining year</strong>, not just the price.</p>

::part What to put in your lease
<p>A good lease protects you for decades. A bad one can make you lose everything in one family argument. <strong>These are the clauses we never sign without:</strong></p>
<div class="vk-steps">
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">1</span><h4>A fixed extension option</h4><p>With the price agreed today (or a clear formula), plus a right of first refusal if the owner ever sells.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">2</span><h4>Permission to build</h4><p>To build, renovate and demolish, and clarity on who owns the buildings during the lease.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">3</span><h4>The right to sublease</h4><p>And to rent commercially (short-term rentals included), and to transfer the lease to a buyer or to your company.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">4</span><h4>Every signature</h4><p>Of everyone with a claim to the land. In Bali, land is often family land. Spouses and heirs should sign or give written consent.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">5</span><h4>Land tax and disputes</h4><p>Who pays land tax (PBB) and who handles disputes with neighbours, access roads and the banjar.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">6</span><h4>Access rights</h4><p>A plot without legal road access is a plot you cannot build or sell properly.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step vk-wide"><span class="vk-n">7</span><h4>The what-ifs</h4><p>What happens if the land is rezoned, expropriated or the owner passes away.</p></div>
</div>
<div class="gold"><span class="gold-k">Pay into escrow</span><p>Pay your deposit (often 10–30%) <strong>into the notary’s escrow account, never directly to the seller,</strong> and only release the rest after the checks are done and the deed is signed.</p></div>

::lesson ch4 | 04 | Zoning | Part 1 · Lesson 4 | Zoning: the colour that | decides everything | What you can build and rent, and when to walk away.
::part What the colours mean
<p>Every piece of land in Bali sits in a zone on the official spatial plan (the <strong>RDTR</strong>, Rencana Detail Tata Ruang). Each zone has a colour on the map, and <strong>that colour decides what you can build and whether you can rent it to tourists.</strong></p>
<div class="tool noshadow zone-pick">
<h5>What can I do on this land?</h5>
<div class="field"><label for="bali_zone">Pick the zone colour of the plot</label><select id="bali_zone" data-field="bali_zone"><option value="">Choose a colour</option><option value="yellow">Yellow (residential)</option><option value="pink">Pink (tourism)</option><option value="orange">Orange (mixed)</option><option value="red">Red (commercial)</option><option value="green">Green (agricultural)</option><option value="protected">Protected / conservation</option></select></div>
<div class="zone-res" data-zone="" hidden><b>Pick a colour</b>The result shows here.</div>
<div class="zone-res" data-zone="yellow" hidden><b><span class="zdot" style="background:#E8C547"></span>Check your exact plot</b>Meant for houses and residential villas, with some small businesses. Commercial short-term rental needs the right licences, and this is where most disputes happen.</div>
<div class="zone-res" data-zone="pink" hidden><b><span class="zdot" style="background:#E3A1B4"></span>Yes, this zone is made for it</b>Meant for hotels, resorts, guesthouses and villas. This is the zone made for tourist accommodation.</div>
<div class="zone-res" data-zone="orange" hidden><b><span class="zdot" style="background:#E39A55"></span>Usually yes, with the right licences</b>Housing combined with commerce and services.</div>
<div class="zone-res" data-zone="red" hidden><b><span class="zdot" style="background:#C2544A"></span>Not intended for villas</b>Meant for shops, offices and restaurants. Check case by case.</div>

<div class="zone-res" data-zone="protected" hidden><b><span class="zdot" style="background:#2F5D4E"></span>No</b>Forests, rivers, coastlines and sacred areas.</div>
</div>
<p>Colours and names can differ slightly between regencies (Badung, Gianyar, Tabanan…), so always read the legend of the map for your area.</p>
<div class="gold"><span class="gold-k">Golden rule</span><p><strong>Never trust the colour a seller tells you.</strong> Check it yourself on the official map and with your notary.</p></div>
::part How to check the zone of a plot
<div class="vk-steps">
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">1</span><h4>Get the details</h4><p>The land certificate number and coordinates from the seller (or the exact location pin).</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">2</span><h4>Check the official map</h4><p>The national spatial planning map (Gistaru, from the Ministry of Agrarian Affairs and Spatial Planning) shows the RDTR zones. For Badung, the BATARA app shows zoning too.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">3</span><h4>Ask for a KKPR check</h4><p>The spatial-use conformity confirmation used in the licensing system. It tells you if your planned activity fits the zone.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">4</span><h4>Visit the offices</h4><p>The land office (BPN) and the village office, to confirm there are no disputes or special restrictions.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step vk-wide"><span class="vk-n">5</span><h4>Check the surroundings</h4><p>Distance to temples (sacred areas have extra restrictions), rivers, the coastline and cliffs.</p></div>
</div>
<p>Two more Bali-wide rules: buildings are limited to <strong>15 metres in height</strong> (traditionally “no higher than a coconut tree”), and land close to temples falls under sacred-area restrictions defined with the Hindu community.</p>
<p class="warn"><strong>After the floods of 2025.</strong> On 10 September 2025, Bali suffered its worst floods in years, with more than a dozen deaths. Days later, Governor Wayan Koster announced a regulation to bar commercial construction such as villas, hotels and restaurants on productive agricultural land. Before you buy anything on or near rice fields, ask your notary how this applies in your regency today.</p>
::part How zones change in practice
<p>Here is something you will not read in most guides: <strong>zone colours in Bali are not set in stone.</strong> Spatial plans are revised periodically. When a regency updates its RDTR, land that was green can become yellow or pink, usually where the area around it has already developed. A green plot next to existing villas, roads and yellow or pink zones is more likely to change colour than a green plot in the middle of the rice terraces.</p>
<p>You will also hear sellers say that “this green land can be changed”, sometimes casually, as if it were a formality. Be very careful here.</p>
<div class="vk-two">
<div class="vk-card vk-yes"><div class="vk-tag">✓ What protects you</div><ul><li>Only an official change: a revised RDTR or a confirmed KKPR, in writing.</li></ul></div>
<div class="vk-card vk-no"><div class="vk-tag">✕ What does not</div><ul><li>Promises, “arrangements” and verbal assurances are not a title. What was tolerated yesterday can be enforced tomorrow.</li><li>Productive rice fields are now the most protected land on the island, especially after the 2025 floods.</li><li>Never pay extra for a future conversion that has not happened yet.</li></ul></div>
</div>
<div class="gold"><span class="gold-k">Our approach</span><p><strong>Price the land for the colour it is today.</strong> If it changes later in an official revision, that is upside. If you need it to change for your plan to work, it is not a deal, it is a bet.</p></div>
<blockquote class="pullquote">“Buy the zone you need. Do not buy the zone you hope for.”</blockquote>

::lesson ch5 | 05 | Your PT PMA | Part 2 · Lesson 5 | Your PT PMA: the company that | lets you do business | The real minimum investment in 2026.
::part What is a PT PMA?
<p>A PT PMA (Perseroan Terbatas Penanaman Modal Asing) is an Indonesian limited company with foreign shareholders. <strong>For a foreigner who wants to run a villa rental business legally in Bali, it is the standard route.</strong></p>
<div class="vk-two">
<div class="vk-card"><h4>[[ic:sparkle]]Earn rental income legally</h4><p>Run a business and earn income in Indonesia as a foreigner.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>[[ic:key]]Hold the licences</h4><p>The NIB and the right KBLI code that booking platforms now ask for.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>[[ic:box]]Hold land rights</h4><p>Like HGB, or sign leases in the company’s name.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>[[ic:calendar]]Sponsor your visa</h4><p>Your investor KITAS, so you can live in Bali and manage your business.</p></div>
</div>
<p>Your PT PMA has at least two shareholders (people or companies), a director and a commissioner, a registered address in Indonesia, a tax number and a bank account. It reports its investment activity every quarter (the LKPM report) and files taxes every month and every year.</p>
<p>It sounds like a lot. <strong>In practice, a good set-up agent and a local accountant do 90% of it for you.</strong> Your job is to choose them well and understand what they are doing.</p>
<p class="warn"><strong>Important:</strong> the company’s business code (KBLI) must match what you actually do. A company set up for “real estate” or “consulting” cannot legally rent villas to tourists (lesson 7).</p>
::part The real minimum investment in 2026
<p>For years, the number you saw everywhere online was IDR 10 billion (around USD 600,000), and many people gave up on Bali because of it. <strong>The rules changed.</strong> Under BKPM Regulation No. 5 of 2025, in force since 2 October 2025, there are two different numbers:</p>
<div class="vk-two">
<div class="vk-stat"><b>IDR 2.5B</b><span><strong>Paid-up capital:</strong> money actually deposited in the company’s bank account (was IDR 10 billion).</span></div>
<div class="vk-stat"><b>IDR 10B+</b><span><strong>Total investment plan:</strong> what the company commits to invest in its business, per business line.</span></div>
</div>
<div class="vk-two">
<div class="vk-card vk-yes"><div class="vk-tag">✓ Good to know</div><ul><li>For property and accommodation businesses, <strong>the value of land and buildings can count towards the investment total.</strong> Your leasehold, construction and furniture are part of your investment.</li><li>The paid-up capital cannot be transferred out for 12 months, but it is not frozen: it can be used for operations, buying assets and funding construction.</li><li>Companies set up under the old rule can apply to reduce their capital to the new minimum.</li></ul></div>
<div class="vk-card"><div class="vk-tag tag-t">What the internet says vs what happens</div><p><strong>Online:</strong> “You need to invest IDR 10 billion to open a company in Bali.” Many people think they must transfer it on day one.</p><p><strong>In practice:</strong> the IDR 10 billion is an investment plan, not a single wire transfer. You declare it at set-up and report progress every quarter through the LKPM. Your lease, construction, furniture and operations build towards it over the years. What needs to be in the account is the paid-up capital, and even that is there to be used for the business.</p></div>
</div>
::part Questions for your set-up agent
<p>Every agent structures this slightly differently, and the details depend on your business code, your share structure and your visa plans. <strong>Before you sign with anyone, ask these five questions:</strong></p>
<div class="vk-steps">
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">1</span><h4>Capital</h4><p>How much do I need to deposit, when, and what can I spend it on?</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">2</span><h4>Investment plan</h4><p>How will the investment plan be realised and reported (LKPM)?</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">3</span><h4>Business codes</h4><p>Which KBLI codes will my company have, and do they allow short-term villa rental?</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">4</span><h4>Visa</h4><p>How does this structure work with the investor KITAS I want?</p></div>
<div class="vk-step vk-wide"><span class="vk-n">5</span><h4>Yearly costs</h4><p>What does it cost every year to keep the company compliant (accounting, tax, LKPM, licences)?</p></div>
</div>

::lesson ch6 | 06 | The investor visa | Part 2 · Lesson 6 | The investor visa (and how | people get deported) | Investor KITAS step by step.
::part Bali is strict about your visa
<p>Let’s be very clear: <strong>a tourist visa does not allow you to do business in Bali.</strong> Not to work in your villa, not to manage staff, not even to promote your villa on social media.</p>
<div class="gold"><span class="gold-k">It happens</span><p>In September 2024, an Australian on a Visa on Arrival was <strong>deported and banned from re-entering Indonesia for six months</strong> for promoting a villa on social media. Officials had been monitoring his posts before they arrested him. In 2026, authorities also stepped up checks on content creators and photographers working on tourist visas.</p></div>
<p>Overstaying, working without the right permit or running a business on the wrong visa can lead to fines, detention, deportation and entry bans. And if your business is in your name and you get deported, you will be managing it from abroad.</p>
<div class="vk-two">
<div class="vk-card vk-yes"><div class="vk-tag">✓ On a tourist visa you can</div><ul><li>Look at properties</li><li>Meet notaries</li><li>Sign a lease</li></ul></div>
<div class="vk-card vk-no"><div class="vk-tag">✕ But not</div><ul><li>Work in your villa or manage staff</li><li>Promote your villa on social media</li><li>Operate the business in any way</li></ul></div>
</div>
<blockquote class="pullquote">“If you would not post it with your immigration officer watching, you are probably on the wrong visa.”</blockquote>
::part The investor KITAS step by step
<p>The right status for most villa investors is the <strong>investor KITAS</strong> (visa index E28A): a temporary stay permit for foreigners who own shares in a PT PMA and sit on its board as a director or commissioner.</p>
<div class="cmp-wrap"><table class="cmp"><thead><tr><th></th><th>What you need to know</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><th>Who</th><td>Shareholders of a PT PMA who are director or commissioner</td></tr>
<tr><th>Minimum shares</th><td>IDR 10 billion of shares in your own name (not through a nominee or holding company)</td></tr>
<tr><th>Validity</th><td>1 or 2 years, renewable</td></tr>
<tr><th>Can you work?</th><td>A director can sign contracts and manage the company. Operational jobs need a separate work permit.</td></tr>
<tr><th>Timing</th><td>About 4–8 weeks after the investment ministry’s recommendation; longer if company documents need changes</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>
<p class="warn"><strong>Two different tests.</strong> The company can be set up with IDR 2.5 billion paid-up capital, but the investor KITAS asks for IDR 10 billion of shares in your name. Ask your agent to plan your share structure with the visa in mind from day one, not afterwards.</p>

::lesson ch7 | 07 | Licences to rent short-term | Part 2 · Lesson 7 | Licences to rent short-term | (the 2026 crackdown) | NIB, KBLI, permits and the 10% tax.
::part What happened in 2025 and 2026
<p>For years, many Bali villas were rented on Airbnb without any licence. <strong>That era is ending.</strong> Here is the timeline:</p>
<div class="tl">
<div class="tl-item"><div class="tl-title"><b>Jul 2025</b> 48 illegal structures demolished at Bingin Beach</div></div>
<div class="tl-item"><div class="tl-title"><b>3 Dec 2025</b> Bali’s governor proposed stopping Airbnb operations</div><div class="tl-body"><div class="tl-text"><p>Citing illegal rentals run by foreigners and lost tax revenue.</p></div></div></div>
<div class="tl-item"><div class="tl-title"><b>Dec 2025</b> Platforms are not banned, but every listing must be licensed</div><div class="tl-body"><div class="tl-text"><p>The Ministry of Tourism made it clear: correctly zoned and paying tax.</p></div></div></div>
<div class="tl-item"><div class="tl-title"><b>Feb 2026</b> Airbnb started asking hosts for their NIB and KBLI</div></div>
<div class="tl-item"><div class="tl-title"><b>31 Mar 2026</b> Deadline announced for listings to be licensed</div></div>
<div class="tl-item"><div class="tl-title"><b>May 2026</b> About 1,600 unlicensed businesses identified nationwide</div><div class="tl-body"><div class="tl-text"><p>Villa registrations were up 76.4% in a year.</p></div></div></div>
<div class="tl-item"><div class="tl-title"><b>1 Aug 2026</b> Planned start of delisting for operators who did not regularise</div></div>
<div class="tl-item"><div class="tl-title"><b>Jun 2027</b> Target for automatic checks of every listing against the national licensing system (OSS)</div></div>
</div>
<p>Nine booking platforms are working with the Ministry of Tourism on this, including Airbnb, Booking.com, Agoda, Expedia and Traveloka. Listings can be restored once the operator is licensed.</p>
::part The licences you need
<p>Requirements depend on your structure, zone and regency, but a legal short-term villa rental in Bali generally needs:</p>
<div class="vk-grid3">
<div class="vk-card"><span class="vk-n">1</span><h4>A legal entity</h4><p>A PT PMA (or another legal entity) that operates the villa.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><span class="vk-n">2</span><h4>NIB</h4><p>The business identification number, issued through the OSS online licensing system.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><span class="vk-n">3</span><h4>The right KBLI</h4><p>Villas (55193), homestays or pondok wisata (55130), other short-term accommodation (55199). Pondok wisata is aimed at local, Indonesian-run homestays and is typically not available to foreign-owned companies.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><span class="vk-n">4</span><h4>NKU &amp; Sertifikat Standar</h4><p>The activity number and standards certificate linked to your business, depending on its risk level.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><span class="vk-n">5</span><h4>PBG &amp; SLF</h4><p>The building approval and the certificate that the building is fit for use (commercial use where required).</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><span class="vk-n">6</span><h4>Correct zoning</h4><p>For tourist accommodation (lesson 4).</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><span class="vk-n">7</span><h4>Local tax</h4><p>Registration (NPWPD) and the <strong>10% hotel and restaurant tax</strong> on your rental revenue, reported and paid every month. Airbnb does not pay it for you.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><span class="vk-n">8</span><h4>Village / banjar</h4><p>Registration, which in many areas is expected and makes daily life much easier.</p></div>
</div>
<div class="vk-tip"><b>Pro tip:</b> when you buy an existing villa, ask for every one of these documents before you sign. A villa that “has been on Airbnb for years” without them is not a proven business. It is a risk.</div>
::part Why stricter rules are good news
<p>It is easy to read all of this and feel discouraged. We see it the other way around. Remember the 34,500 listings from lesson 1? A part of them are not licensed. <strong>As enforcement tightens, a share of your competition will simply disappear.</strong> The hosts who stay will have:</p>
<div class="chips"><span>✓ Less competition for the same tourists</span><span>✓ Better visibility and rankings</span><span>✓ Access to all booking channels, corporate travellers and agencies</span><span>✓ A business they can actually sell one day</span><span>✓ Peace of mind: no closure notice, no visit from immigration</span></div>
<p>If Bali gets even stricter in the coming years, and everything suggests it will, a fully legal villa becomes something rare. And rare things are valuable.</p>
<blockquote class="pullquote">“A legal villa in a strict market is not a cost. It is your moat.”</blockquote>

::lesson ch8 | 08 | Buying a finished villa | Part 3 · Lesson 8 | Buying a finished villa | without buying a problem | Built-to-sell villas, rice-field fill and sinking floors.
::part Built to sell, not to last
<p>Walk around Canggu, Pererenan or Uluwatu and you will see villas going up everywhere. Many of them are built by developers whose business model is simple: <strong>build fast, build cheap, sell fast.</strong></p>
<p>There is nothing wrong with a developer making a profit. The problem is the incentive: the builder needs the villa to look perfect on the day of the sale and to survive the warranty period, if there is one. After that, it is your problem. In our experience, a cheaply built Bali villa often <strong>looks amazing in year one, starts showing problems in year two, and needs serious money by year three.</strong> We have seen:</p>
<div class="mk-grid">
<div class="mk"><span class="mk-x">✕</span><h4>[[ic:alert]]Cracks</h4><p>In walls and floors a few months after handover.</p></div>
<div class="mk"><span class="mk-x">✕</span><h4>[[ic:refresh]]Leaking roofs</h4><p>In the first real rainy season.</p></div>
<div class="mk"><span class="mk-x">✕</span><h4>[[ic:box]]Pools that lose water</h4><p>Every day, because of poor waterproofing.</p></div>
<div class="mk"><span class="mk-x">✕</span><h4>[[ic:wrench]]Rusting steel</h4><p>Reinforcement pushing concrete apart, especially near the sea.</p></div>
<div class="mk"><span class="mk-x">✕</span><h4>[[ic:key]]Swollen doors and windows</h4><p>That stop closing, made from untreated wood.</p></div>
<div class="mk"><span class="mk-x">✕</span><h4>[[ic:sparkle]]Mould behind fresh paint</h4><p>Because walls were never properly waterproofed.</p></div>
<div class="mk"><span class="mk-x">✕</span><h4>[[ic:bulb]]Weak electrics</h4><p>Too weak for air conditioning in every room.</p></div>
</div>
<p>These are not just repair costs. They are bad reviews, cancelled bookings and lost months while you fix things. <strong>A villa with a leak in the bedroom does not get five stars, whatever the design looks like.</strong></p>
::part The rice field under your villa
<p>A huge part of the land being built on in Bali was, until recently, rice fields. Rice fields are wet, soft and designed to hold water. To build on them, the land is filled and raised. When that fill is done properly (the right material, compacted in layers, with time to settle and proper drainage) it can work. When it is done fast and cheap, you get:</p>
<div class="vk-two">
<div class="vk-card"><h4>Ground settlement</h4><p>Parts of the villa sink unevenly. Floors tilt, tiles crack, walls split, doors stop closing.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>Drainage problems</h4><p>The plot sits lower than the neighbours or the road, and water flows into your villa in heavy rain.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>Pool and terrace movement</h4><p>Heavy structures on poorly compacted ground move first.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>Humidity and mould</h4><p>From a high water table.</p></div>
</div>
<p class="warn">Even if the plot was converted legally years ago, the soil underneath is still the same. Ask questions.</p>
<div class="checklist">
<label class="task soft"><input type="checkbox" data-check="bali-rf-1"><span>Was this land a rice field? When was it filled, with what, and how was it compacted?</span></label>
<label class="task soft"><input type="checkbox" data-check="bali-rf-2"><span>Were soil tests done? Ask for the report and the foundation design.</span></label>
<label class="task soft"><input type="checkbox" data-check="bali-rf-3"><span>Is the villa higher than the road and neighbouring fields?</span></label>
<label class="task soft"><input type="checkbox" data-check="bali-rf-4"><span>Where does rainwater go? Look for drainage channels and their size.</span></label>
<label class="task soft"><input type="checkbox" data-check="bali-rf-5"><span>Visit during or right after heavy rain if you possibly can.</span></label>
</div>
::part How to inspect a villa before you sign
<p>Never buy a villa in Bali based on photos and a tour with the seller. Here is the process we follow:</p>
<div class="vk-steps">
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">1</span><h4>Documents first</h4><p>Land certificate, lease, zoning, PBG and SLF, as-built drawings, list of materials, warranties and the contractor’s name.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">2</span><h4>Your own inspector</h4><p>An independent building inspector or structural engineer, never the seller’s. It costs very little compared to what it can save you.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">3</span><h4>Structure</h4><p>Cracks (especially diagonal ones), uneven floors, signs of settlement, exposed or rusty steel.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">4</span><h4>Water</h4><p>Roof, gutters, waterproofing of bathrooms and terraces, damp patches, smell of mould, fresh paint in strange places.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">5</span><h4>The pool</h4><p>Ask for a water-loss test over several days.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">6</span><h4>Materials</h4><p>Real hardwood or treated timber? Quality of windows, roof tiles, plumbing and electrical cables.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">7</span><h4>Systems</h4><p>Electricity capacity (kVA), septic tank, water source and pressure, hot water, Wi-Fi.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">8</span><h4>Neighbours &amp; banjar</h4><p>Floods, disputes, construction planned next door.</p></div>

::lesson ch9 | 09 | Building your own villa | Part 3 · Lesson 9 | Build your own villa so it lasts | 20 years, not 2 | The rules we would give our best friend.
::part Build it right from the ground up
<p>Building gives you full control over quality, design and layout. It also gives you full responsibility. These are the rules we would give our best friend:</p>
<div class="vk-steps">
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">1</span><h4>Permits before the first brick</h4><p>Your PBG must match the zone and your plans. Building first and “legalising later” is exactly what Bali is cracking down on.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">2</span><h4>Start with the soil</h4><p>A soil test and a foundation designed by an engineer are not optional, especially on former rice fields.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">3</span><h4>Design the drainage first</h4><p>Raise the plot above the road, plan where every drop of rain goes, and size gutters for tropical downpours.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">4</span><h4>Materials for the climate, not the photo</h4><p>Treated or durable hardwood, stainless or galvanised fittings near the sea, quality waterproofing in every wet area, good ventilation to fight humidity.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">5</span><h4>Plan electricity early</h4><p>Agree the capacity with your utility (PLN) early. Upgrades can take time.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">6</span><h4>Pay by milestones</h4><p>Never upfront. Link every payment to finished, inspected work and keep a retention (often 5–10%) until after the first rainy season.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">7</span><h4>Your own eyes on site</h4><p>An architect, project manager or inspector who works for you, not for the contractor, and checks every stage.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">8</span><h4>Height and setbacks</h4><p>Respect the 15 m height limit and setbacks from roads, rivers and neighbours.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step vk-wide"><span class="vk-n">9</span><h4>Schedule around the rain</h4><p>Foundations and roofs in the dry season if you can.</p></div>
</div>
<div class="gold"><span class="gold-k">Plan for delays</span><p><strong>Building in Bali almost always takes longer than promised.</strong> Plan your cash flow for delays and do not book guests before the villa is finished and inspected.</p></div>

::lesson ch11 | 11 | Run it day to day | Part 3 · Lesson 11 | Running a villa in Bali | day to day | Water, waste, rainy season, staff and ceremonies.
::part Water: filter everything
<p><strong>Bali tap water is not drinkable.</strong> Most villas get their water from the local utility (PDAM), from a deep well, or from water trucks in drier areas like the Bukit. Quality varies by area, by season and even by property. After heavy rain it becomes less predictable, and Bali groundwater is also hard (full of calcium and magnesium).</p>
<p>This affects your guests’ health, your reviews, your pool and your appliances. Our advice: install a proper water filtration system in every villa. A typical set-up, chosen after testing your water:</p>
<div class="vk-steps">
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">1</span><h4>Sediment filter</h4><p>Removes sand, dirt and rust.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">2</span><h4>Activated carbon</h4><p>Removes smells and many impurities.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">3</span><h4>Water softener</h4><p>Protects showers, taps, washing machines and water heaters from limescale.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step"><span class="vk-n">4</span><h4>UV purification</h4><p>Neutralises bacteria and viruses.</p></div>
<div class="vk-step vk-wide"><span class="vk-n">5</span><h4>Reverse osmosis in the kitchen</h4><p>For drinking water, or 19-litre water gallons with a dispenser.</p></div>
</div>
<div class="bubble"><span class="bub-from">In your welcome notes</span>We’ve installed a high-quality water filter, so you can shower with clean, skin-friendly water and brush your teeth safely.</div>
<div class="vk-tip"><b>Pro tip:</b> schedule filter changes and a water test with your maintenance team. A filter system that is not maintained is just decoration.</div>
::part Waste: the problem nobody shows you
<p>Bali has a serious waste problem, and as a villa owner it is your problem too. The island’s biggest landfill, Suwung in Denpasar, served Denpasar, Badung, Gianyar and Tabanan. From 31 March 2026 it only accepted inorganic and residual waste, and it was scheduled to close completely on 1 August 2026, with a waste-to-energy plant under construction to replace it. <strong>During the transition, uncollected trash piled up on roadsides in parts of the island for weeks.</strong></p>
<p>The rainy season makes collection harder: flooded roads, delayed pick-ups, bins that overflow and attract animals and smells. Nothing ruins a five-star stay faster than a pile of trash at the gate.</p>
<div class="tiles">
<div><b>[[ic:calendar]]A reliable private collector</b>With a fixed schedule, and a backup.</div>
<div><b>[[ic:recycle]]Separate organic and inorganic</b>Compost organic waste on site if you have the space.</div>
<div><b>[[ic:box]]Closed, animal-proof bins</b>Stored out of sight, away from the entrance.</div>
<div><b>[[ic:sparkle]]Cut plastic</b>Refillable water, bulk amenities, no single-use bottles.</div>
<div class="vk-wide"><b>[[ic:phone]]Explain the system to guests</b>In your welcome notes. Many come to Bali for its nature, and a villa that handles waste responsibly is part of the experience.</div>
</div>
::part Rainy season, power &amp; maintenance
<p>The rainy season runs roughly from November to March. It is green, dramatic and beautiful, and <strong>it tests every weakness in your villa.</strong></p>
<div class="vk-grid3">
<div class="vk-card"><h4>Before the rains</h4><p>Clean gutters and drains, check the roof, reseal terraces and bathrooms, trim trees near the roof and power lines.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>During the rains</h4><p>Watch for leaks, flooding in the garden, pool overflow and algae. Have your maintenance person on call.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>Humidity &amp; mould</h4><p>Run dehumidifiers or AC regularly in closed rooms, air out wardrobes, use breathable materials, treat wood.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>Power cuts</h4><p>Keep rechargeable lights, consider a backup for Wi-Fi and the fridge, and make sure guests know what to do.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>Pests</h4><p>Regular termite and pest control, especially for wooden structures and roofs.</p></div>
</div>
<p>Build a monthly maintenance routine with your team (the course has the inspection and maintenance checklists) and add these Bali-specific items to it.</p>
<div class="vk-tip"><b>Pro tip:</b> adjust your pricing for the low season instead of fighting it. Monthly stays for nomads and long-stay guests can keep occupancy up from January to March.</div>
::part Your team, your banjar &amp; Nyepi
<p>In Bali, your local relationships are part of your business plan.</p>
<div class="vk-two">
<div class="vk-card"><h4>Your team</h4><p>Most villas have a small local team (housekeeping, pool and garden, maintenance) and a manager or management company. Treat them well, pay on time, follow labour rules and give them clear SOPs. <strong>Good staff in Bali are worth gold and stay with good owners.</strong></p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>Ceremonies</h4><p>Balinese Hinduism is part of daily life. Your staff will need days off for ceremonies, and some will place offerings at the villa. Respect it and plan your schedule around it.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>Your banjar</h4><p>The village council manages local matters, from security to community contributions. Introduce yourself, contribute when asked and keep a good relationship. When you need help, they are the first to know.</p></div>
<div class="vk-card"><h4>Nyepi, the Day of Silence</h4><p>Once a year, the whole island stops for 24 hours. No lights, no noise, no going out, and the airport closes. Tell guests booked on those dates well in advance and prepare food and a cosy kit. <strong>For many, it becomes the highlight of their trip.</strong></p></div>
</div>
<p class="warn"><strong>Taxes every month:</strong> remember the 10% local tax on your rental income. Your manager or accountant should report and pay it monthly.</p>
<blockquote class="pullquote">“Bali rewards the owners who become part of the community, not just part of the skyline.”</blockquote>
