Legal setup: private, self-employed or company?
@@485
Let’s be real, no one gets excited about legal paperwork. Luckily, you don’t need a complicated legal setup to get started. But it’s important to know your options and to declare your income from day one.
@@486
You have three main options: {1}rent out as a private individual, start as self-employed, or register a company.{2} Which one fits depends on your country, what you offer your guests and how big you want to grow.
@@487
Option 1: rent out as a private individual
@@488
Many people don’t know this: {1}in many countries you can rent out a place as a private person, without registering as self-employed or opening a company.{2} You simply declare the rental income on your personal tax return once a year, and you can usually deduct costs like rent, utilities, furniture and cleaning.
@@489
This usually works when:
@@490
{1}You only rent out the space.{2} Cleaning between guests, keys and basic maintenance are normal for any landlord.
@@491
{1}You don’t offer hotel-like services during the stay,{2} such as a reception, daily cleaning, meals, laundry or airport transfers.
@@492
{1}You keep it small:{2} one or a few listings, and no staff on your payroll.
@@493
Once you start offering hotel-like services or grow into a real operation with staff, tax authorities usually see it as a business. Then you need to register as self-employed or open a company, and in some countries you also charge VAT.
@@494
Example: how it works
@@495
Spain
@@496
If you rent out an apartment you own without hotel-like services, you declare it as rental income in your yearly income tax return (IRPF). No need to become autónomo. If you sublet a place you rent (rental arbitrage), the income is also taxed in your yearly return, unless it’s run as a business. Offer hotel-like services and it becomes a business: you register as autónomo and usually charge 10% VAT.
@@497
Belgium
@@498
If you rent out one or a few places without extra services, you can do it as a private individual. You declare it on your yearly tax return: for the property, tax is based on its cadastral income ({1}kadastraal inkomen{2}), and if it’s furnished, 40% of the rent counts as income from the furniture. Offer services like breakfast or cleaning during the stay, and do it regularly? Then it becomes a professional activity: you register as self-employed (this can be a side activity, {3}zelfstandige in bijberoep{4}) and usually charge 6% VAT.
@@499
United States
@@500
Most hosts report their rental income as rental income on Schedule E, without self-employment tax. If you provide substantial services, like daily cleaning, meals or concierge, it counts as a business (Schedule C).
@@501
{1}Always declare your income.{2} Since 2023, platforms like Airbnb and Booking.com share hosts’ income with the tax authorities for every listing in the EU. Renting out as a private individual doesn’t mean tax-free: it just means less admin.
