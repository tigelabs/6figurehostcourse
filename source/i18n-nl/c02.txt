Understanding local rules
@@447
{1}Rules around property rentals vary widely depending on where you are, but this also offers opportunities. {2}In general, cities with more tourism tend to have stricter regulations due to housing shortages, tourism limits, and local government control.
@@448
Cities like Barcelona, Paris, New York City, and Amsterdam often require licenses, limit the number of rental nights, or restrict short-term rentals to certain zones.
@@449
{1}That doesn't mean strictly regulated places are a no-go.{2} We currently run multiple successful apartments in Barcelona, one of the most difficult cities for Airbnb. They don’t have a tourist license, but they’re all still legal.
@@450
{1}In Barcelona, anyone is allowed to create a listing on a platform like Airbnb without a license, as long as you host stays of 31 nights or more.{2} Not many people know this, and that’s exactly the opportunity.
@@451
While monthly stays tend to generate less income per night than short-term bookings, they’re still highly profitable. Plus, they come with fewer guest turnovers, less day-to-day management, and more stability. You might not even need a manager or a full-time team.
@@452
On the other hand, {1}some areas are much more flexible and easier to start in.{2} Places like Bali, parts of Mexico, Portugal, and Southern Spain are known for being more open to short-term rentals, especially in tourist-friendly zones or when offering longer stays. This is why choosing the right location is key.
@@453
Before you list any property, it's crucial to understand the local laws and regulations in your area. Every city has its own rules when it comes to short-term rentals. Some require specific licenses or permits, others have restrictions on the number of nights you can rent, and some zones may not allow short-term rentals at all.
