@@1615
Add clear house rules to your listing.

Private vs. shared, in writing

Lockable from inside and outside. Non-negotiable for safety, privacy and trust.
@@1620
Dedicated, labeled spaces
@@1621
Label shelves, drawers and even fridge sections in shared kitchens and bathrooms.

Clean more often
@@1623

Keep extras

The 3-step decluttering process for shared spaces
@@1631
Over time, shared kitchens and bathrooms fill up with things previous guests left behind. That means mess, confusion and even hygiene issues. {1}Reset and declutter at least every 3 months{2}, without removing items that are still in use. First, pick your method:
@@1632
Method A
@@1633
The “lost &amp; found” system
@@1634
Choose one spot for leftover items, like a basket in the bathroom or a freezer drawer. Anything placed there is removed after a set deadline. Great for shampoo bottles, freezer food and forgotten belongings.
@@1635
Method B
@@1636
The label system
@@1637
Leave tape and a marker near shared storage (fridge door, pantry). Guests label their items with their name. Anything unlabeled gets cleared out.
@@1638
Key areas:
@@1639
Shower bottles
@@1640
Freezer &amp; fridge door
@@1641
Unlabeled drawers &amp; shelves
@@1642
Random items in common areas
@@1643
Start with a deep clean
@@1644
Clean the kitchen, bathroom and hallway. It’s the best moment to spot forgotten items, expired food and things taking up space. Then move them to your removal spot, or start labeling.
@@1645
Communicate clearly and kindly
@@1646
Even if you spoke to someone in person, send a message with the method and a short deadline. Send a reminder the day before you remove anything.
@@1647
Set a clear removal date
@@1648
Give people 48–72 hours to claim their things, then remove what’s left. Optional: let remaining items be “adopted” before they’re thrown away.
