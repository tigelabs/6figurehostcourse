Pro tips for closing the deal
@@927
{1}Speak their language.{2} Don’t sell the dream of hosting. Sell peace of mind. Focus on how you’ll take care of their property, pay rent on time, and be the best tenant they’ve ever had.
@@928
{1}Use social proof.{2} Show examples of your listings, reviews or experience. If you’re new, share what you’ve learned and your plan. Confidence builds trust.
@@929
{1}Show that you know the rules.{2} Explain exactly what’s allowed in their area (for example mid-term stays of 31+ nights) and how you’ll follow it. Owners relax when they see you’ve done your homework.
@@930
{1}Show your systems.{2} Bring the tools from this course: your property manual, cleaning and inspection checklists, contracts for your team and your house rules. It shows you run this professionally.
@@931
{1}Offer a win-win.{2} Think about what the landlord wants most: no stress, steady income, a well-kept property. Your offer should check those boxes clearly.
@@932
{1}Know your stuff.{2} The more confident you are, and the better you know the official rules and how everything works, the more owners will trust you.
@@933
{1}Always get it in writing.{2} Once they say yes, have a simple agreement ready to sign. It protects both sides and shows you’re serious.
@@934
Put it in writing
@@935
A yes only counts once it’s on paper. What needs to be in writing depends on your business model:
@@936
{1}Rental arbitrage: add a subletting clause to your rental contract.{2} Your contract must clearly say that you’re allowed to sublet the property. Without that clause, subletting isn’t permitted, even if the owner agreed in person. You can use this as a starting point:
@@937
“The landlord authorizes the tenant to sublet the property, in whole or in part, to third parties for short-term and mid-term stays, and to advertise it on online rental platforms. The tenant remains fully responsible for paying the rent and for the condition of the property.”
@@938
Ask a local lawyer or your rental agency to check the wording, because contract rules are different in every country.
@@939
{1}Co-hosting:{2} you don’t sign a rental contract. Instead, sign a co-hosting agreement with the owner that covers your tasks, your commission, who pays for what, and how either of you can end it.
@@940
{1}Buying a property:{2} you don’t need anyone’s permission to rent it out, but check the building or homeowners’ association rules and the local license rules {3}before{4} you complete the purchase.
