Pricing strategies
@@1170
to stay competitive from day one
@@1171
Setting the right price is one of the most important factors for your success, especially when you're just getting started. If your price is too high, your listing won’t get traction. Too low, and you might fill your calendar but leave money on the table.
@@1172
Here’s how to price smart from day one:
@@1173
1. Start lower to build momentum
@@1174
When your listing is new and doesn’t have reviews yet, guests are taking a small risk by booking. To overcome that, offer a competitive introductory price (10–20% below market average). This helps you get your first few bookings and collect 5-star reviews faster which boosts your search ranking.
@@1175
2. Use dynamic pricing tools
@@1176
Tools like PriceLabs, Wheelhouse, or Airbnb’s Smart Pricing adjust your nightly rate based on demand, seasonality, events, and competitor pricing. These tools help you avoid undercharging on busy weekends or high seasons.
@@1177
Pro tip: Airbnb’s Smart Pricing tends to undervalue your listing. Use it with caution and always set a minimum price you’re comfortable with.
@@1178
3. Research competitors
@@1179
Research similar listings in your area. Look at:
@@1180
Their nightly rate
@@1181
Cleaning fees
@@1182
Availability
@@1183
Amenities offered
@@1184
Reviews
@@1185
If they’re booked out and you’re not, it’s likely a pricing (or photo) issue. Adjust accordingly.
@@1186
4. Offer weekly &amp; monthly discounts
@@1187
To encourage longer stays and fill your calendar, set smart discounts for weekly and monthly bookings. This can help increase occupancy and reduce turnover work, especially during slower seasons.
@@1188
5. Don’t rely on just the nightly rate
@@1189
Your cleaning fee and minimum stay also impact how attractive your listing is. If your cleaning fee is too high compared to your nightly rate, it might drive guests away, especially for short stays.
@@1190
6. Raise your price strategically
@@1191
Once you have at least 5 strong reviews and some social proof, you can gradually increase your prices. Don’t make a big jump overnight; test small increases and monitor your bookings.
@@1192
7. Use discounts the right way
@@1193
Airbnb offers tools like “early bird” discounts, “last-minute” discounts, and promotions for new listings. Use them strategically to stay booked without cheapening your brand.
@@1194
Final note
@@1195
Pricing is never “set it and forget it.” Revisit your strategy every week or two, especially in the beginning. Stay flexible, use data, and optimize based on results, not just assumptions.
@@1196
With the right pricing strategy, you can stay competitive, attract quality guests, and build up your income from the very first listing.

Setting expectations early
@@1223
Setting clear expectations upfront is one of the most effective ways to avoid misunderstandings, prevent complaints, and ensure a smooth experience for both you and your guests.
@@1224
Before a guest even books, your listing should clearly communicate exactly what they’re getting. This includes things like:
@@1225
Whether the space is private or shared
@@1226
Quiet hours and noise potential
@@1227
Rules about visitors, smoking, or pets
@@1228
Layout of the space and shared areas
@@1229
Location details (e.g., if it’s in a lively area or a quiet neighborhood)
@@1230
Any limitations or quirks (e.g., steep stairs, construction nearby, no elevator)
@@1231
It might feel like you’re pointing out negatives, but transparency builds trust. It also protects you. If issues come up, you can always point back to what was clearly mentioned before booking.
@@1232
Here’s where you should reinforce expectations:
@@1233
Your listing description and house rules
@@1234
The welcome message right after booking
@@1235
Signage or reminders in the property (when necessary)
@@1236
It’s okay to repeat the same things several times, especially if guests share common spaces.
@@1237
Being honest doesn’t scare good guests away. It filters out the ones who aren’t the right fit, and those are often the ones who create problems. Setting expectations early makes your life easier, your reviews better, and your hosting stress-free.
@@1238
How to make guests follow your rules: make them clear
@@1239
{1}Write rules that are specific.{2} “Check-out is strictly at 11:00 AM” works. “Please be respectful” does not.
@@1240
{1}Put them in your house rules.{2} Guests have to accept them before booking, so you can always refer back to them.
@@1241
{1}Repeat them.{2} In the listing description, in the welcome message, and on signs or labels inside the property.
@@1242
{1}Keep them short.{2} Guests are tired when traveling and ignore long texts.
@@1243
{1}Be polite but firm when a rule is broken.{2} Point to the rule they agreed to.
@@1244
Example: shared spaces
@@1245
We mention in our listing seven times that the kitchen and pool are shared, plus one final reminder in the welcome message. And still, occasionally, a guest arrives claiming we didn't mention it and asks for a full refund.
@@1246
The reality is: some people simply don’t read. But that’s their responsibility, not yours. As long as you’ve clearly stated it in multiple places, you’re fully protected. If you can prove it was communicated upfront, guests will accept that they made a mistake, and Airbnb will side with you.
