806|Tools like Pricelabs, Wheelhouse, or Airbnb’s Smart Pricing adjust your nightly rate based on demand, seasonality, events, and competitor pricing. These tools help you avoid undercharging on busy weekends or high seasons.
807|Pro tip: Airbnb’s Smart Pricing tends to undervalue your listing. Use it with caution and always set a minimum price you’re comfortable with.

809|Research similar listings in your area. Look at:
810|Their nightly rate
811|Cleaning fees
812|Availability
813|Amenities offered
814|Reviews
815|If they’re booked out and you’re not, it’s likely a pricing (or photo) issue. Adjust accordingly.
816|4. Offer weekly &amp; monthly discounts
817|To encourage longer stays and fill your calendar, set smart discounts for weekly and monthly bookings. This can help increase occupancy and reduce turnover work especially during slower seasons.
818|5. Don’t rely on just the nightly rate
819|Your cleaning fee and minimum stay also impact how attractive your listing is. If your cleaning fee is too high compared to your nightly rate, it might drive guests away, especially for short stays.
820|6. Raise your price strategically
821|Once you have at least 5 strong reviews and some social proof, you can gradually increase your prices. Don’t make a big jump overnight, test small increases and monitor your bookings.
822|7. Use discounts the right way
823|Airbnb offers tools like “early bird” discounts, “last-minute” discounts, and promotions for new listings. Use them strategically to stay booked without cheapening your brand.
824|Final note
825|Pricing is never “set it and forget it.” Revisit your strategy every week or two, especially in the beginning. Stay flexible, use data, and optimize based on results, not just assumptions.
826|With the right pricing strategy, you can stay competitive, attract quality guests, and build up your income from the very first listing.

858|Setting expectations early
859|Setting clear expectations upfront is one of the most effective ways to avoid misunderstandings, prevent complaints, and ensure a smooth experience for both you and your guests.
860|Before a guest even books, your listing should clearly communicate exactly what they’re getting. This includes things like:
861|Whether the space is private or shared
862|Quiet hours and noise potential
863|Rules about visitors, smoking, or pets
864|Layout of the space and shared areas
865|Location details (e.g., if it’s in a lively area or a quiet neighborhood)
866|Any limitations or quirks (e.g., steep stairs, construction nearby, no elevator)
867|It might feel like you’re pointing out negatives, but transparency builds trust. It also protects you. If issues come up, you can always point back to what was clearly mentioned before booking.
868|Here’s where you should reinforce expectations:
869|Your listing description and house rules
870|The welcome message right after booking
871|Signage or reminders in the property (when necessary)
872|It's okay to repeat the same things multiple times. Especially if your space has shared common spaces with other guests.
873|Being honest doesn’t scare good guests away. It filters out the ones who aren’t the right fit and those are often the ones that create problems. Setting expectations early makes your life easier, your reviews better, and your hosting stress-free.
874|How to make guests follow your rules: make them clear
875|<strong>Write rules that are specific.</strong> “Check-out is strictly at 11:00 AM” works. “Please be respectful” does not.
876|<strong>Put them in your house rules.</strong> Guests have to accept them before booking, so you can always refer back to them.
877|<strong>Repeat them.</strong> In the listing description, in the welcome message, and on signs or labels inside the property.
878|<strong>Keep them short.</strong> Guests are tired when traveling and ignore long texts.
879|<strong>Be polite but firm when a rule is broken.</strong> Point to the rule they agreed to.
880|Example: shared spaces
881|We mention in our listing seven times that the kitchen and pool are shared, plus one final reminder in the welcome message. nd still, occasionally, a guest arrives claiming we didn't mention it and asks for a full refund.
882|The reality is: some people simply don’t read. But that’s their responsibility, not yours. As long as you’ve clearly stated it in multiple places, you’re fully protected. If you can prove it was communicated upfront, guests will accept that they made a mistake. and Airbnb will side with you.

885|In “About this place”
886|Example: construction noise
887|If there's something that could affect the guest experience like construction noise nearby frame it as a benefit rather than a problem. For example, mention in your listing that the current rate already includes a special discount due to temporary construction in the area.
888|This way, guests feel like they’re getting a deal. Often, you don’t even need to reduce your price significantly. Just highlighting that a discount has been applied makes guests think, “I’m getting a great offer,” rather than focusing on the inconvenience. It's all about how you position it.

891|How to handle complaints or damages
892|No matter how well you prepare, at some point, there will always be guests that have complaints or cause damage. What separates great hosts from the rest is how you respond.
893|Step 1: Stay calm and respond fast
894|Always reply quickly and professionally. Even if the complaint feels unfair or minor, take it seriously. Acknowledge the issue, thank the guest for letting you know, and let them know you're working on a solution.
895|Example: "Thank you for sharing this with us. We're so sorry for the inconvenience and will do everything we can to make it right."
896|Step 2: Evaluate and take action
897|For minor complaints, offer a solution quickly (e.g., sending the cleaner again, providing a small refund, or replacing something missing).
898|For bigger issues, gather evidence and act accordingly. This includes checking security footage, messaging logs, cleaner reports, or visiting the property if you're nearby.
899|Step 3: Document everything. Especially pictures are important!
900|Always keep screenshots, pictures, and receipts. If damage occurs, take clear photos and file a damage claim through Airbnb within 14 days or before the next guest checks in, whichever comes first.
901|IMPORTANT: Instruct your cleaner to always take photos if something is damaged or missing (even towels). A quick photo of the room before and after each turnover can be your best proof in case of disputes.
902|Step 4: Be firm when needed
903|If a guest breaks a house rule or causes damage, be polite but direct. Reference the listing and house rules they agreed to when booking. This helps you stay professional and protected.
904|Example: "As stated in the house rules, parties are not allowed. Due to the disturbance and cleanup required, we’ll need to charge an additional fee to cover the extra cleaning and guest compensation."
905|Step 5: Don’t take it personally
906|Not all reviews will be 5 stars, and not every guest will behave perfectly. Learn from each case, improve your systems, and keep going. Over time, your operations will get smoother, and you’ll face fewer problems.
907|Some guests will react unfairly, but it’s important to remember that you never know what someone might be going through. They could be dealing with personal struggles, mental health challenges, or simply the stress of being out of their comfort zone. Traveling can make people tired, anxious, or irritable.
908|Arguing, even when you’re completely right, rarely leads to a good outcome. One of the best things you can do is meet frustration with empathy.
909|<strong>A golden rule that has never failed us: kill them with kindness. </strong>Always acknowledge their experience, express understanding, and let them know you’ll do your best to help. This simple shift in tone can instantly calm a tense situation and turn a potential conflict into a positive review.
910|Handling complaints or damages is part of the business. The faster and more professionally you respond, the better your reviews and your peace of mind will be.
911|At the end of this chapter, you’ll get access to a ready-to-use 'Quick Replies Library' with message templates to handle common issues. You can copy and paste them directly to save time and respond professionally in any situation.
912|How to be a 5-star host
913|Being a 5-star host doesn’t mean being available 24/7 or bending over backward for every request. It means creating a seamless, thoughtful experience for your guests; before, during, and after their stay. Great customer service is often what separates a 4-star review from a 5-star one, and what brings guests back or earns you referrals.
914|before check-in: proactive communication
915|Send a warm, clear pre-arrival message with all necessary info: address, check-in time, how to get there, and how to enter (see bonus templates)
916|Ask for their estimated arrival time and if they have any special needs.
917|Anticipate questions by including WiFi info, local tips, and what to expect during their stay.
918|Be friendly, but professional. You’re setting the tone.
919|During the stay: stay available but not intrusive
920|Check in with them after the first night. A simple “Hope everything’s going well!
921|Let me know if you need anything” goes a long way.
922|Make sure your welcome booklet or digital guide is clear and includes answers to FAQs.
923|If a problem arises, respond fast. Even if you can’t solve it immediately, acknowledge the issue and keep the guest informed.
924|after check-out: leave a lasting impression
925|Thank them for staying and invite them to leave a review.
926|If there was a problem that you solved, mention it (e.g., “Thanks again for your patience with the plumbing issue, we’re glad we could sort it quickly for you!”).
927|Tips for 5-star service:
928|Be human. A warm tone goes further than robotic professionalism.
929|Be clear. Clear instructions and expectations reduce stress.
930|Be honest. If something goes wrong, own it and offer a solution.
931|Be kind. Especially when dealing with complaints, empathy always wins.
932|Customer service isn’t just about fixing problems. It’s about showing your guests that you care. When they feel cared for, they leave happier, review better, and are more likely to recommend your place to others.
