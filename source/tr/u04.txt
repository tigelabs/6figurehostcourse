626|Design does matter.
627|In competitive markets, guests scroll fast. If your place doesn’t pop, it gets skipped. A well-designed space with a beautiful interior can actually increase your nightly rate by 20–50%, and help you stay booked even in low season.
628|That doesn’t mean you need to buy expensive furniture or hire a stylist. You just need visual coherence and good lighting:
629|1. Choose a clear style.
630|Boho, minimalist, modern, industrial. Pick one and stay consistent. This helps your space feel intentional and cohesive.
631|2. Stick to a color palette.
632|Use 2–3 main colors (neutrals + one accent). It creates a calm, organized look that’s easy on the eyes.
633|3. Add 1–2 statement pieces.
634|Think: a bold headboard, unique lighting, a large mirror, or a piece of art. These anchor the space visually and create that wow factor.
635|4. Use textiles to add warmth.
636|Throws, rugs, curtains, and pillows make a space feel cozy and layered even if your furniture is basic.
637|5. Lighting is everything.
638|Use warm light bulbs, multiple light sources (not just ceiling lights), and add lamps to create a cozy, flattering glow. Good lighting makes your photos look 10x better.
639|Even with a minimal budget, you can create a space that feels elevated and intentional. And that’s what gets clicks, bookings, and five-star reviews.
640|Guest-proof your space
641|Here’s the truth: things will break, guests will test your systems, and the more your business grows, the more moving parts you’ll have. That’s why guest-proofing your space and setting it up for easy, low-stress management is key.
642|You want a place that runs like a well-oiled machine, even when you’re on the other side of the world.
643|1. Choose durable over delicate
644|Skip fragile items, high-maintenance fabrics, and anything too precious.
645|Go for:
646|Stain-resistant bedding (maybe beige instead of white and washable)
647|Easy-to-clean furniture and rugs (get a couch cover)
648|Hard-to-break plates, cups, and glasses (especially wine glasses break easily)
649|Wall-mounted lamps or nightstands to avoid clutter and accidents
650|Stainless steel pots and pans that are scratch-resistant
651|2. Think like a cleaner
652|Ask yourself: “How quickly can this place be cleaned and reset for the next guest?”
653|Use minimal decor so surfaces are easy to wipe
654|Avoid open shelves that collect dust
655|Store backup supplies in a locked cabinet for restocking
656|Use labeled baskets or bins for towels, sheets, and essentials
657|If your cleaner can find everything fast and finish without messaging you, you’re winning.
658|3. Anchor everything
659|Secure furniture to the wall, especially in family-friendly units. Tape down rug corners. Use non-slip pads. Avoid anything that tips easily or looks tempting to move.
660|4. Keep it simple for guests
661|The less they have to guess, the better.
662|Use signs or frames to show how to use appliances
663|Include a clear house manual
664|Automate check-in and check-out instructions
665|Place Wi-Fi codes and emergency info somewhere obvious
666|Fewer questions = fewer headaches.
667|5. Lock up what’s off-limits
668|Have a locked owner’s closet for tools, backup stock, and valuables. If you don’t want it touched, don’t leave it out. Simple.
669|6. Bonus: Smart setups save time
670|Use key boxes or smart locks to avoid key handovers
671|Install motion-sensor lights in hallways or outdoor spaces
672|Label drawers and cabinets if needed
673|Your goal is to create a space that feels easy to live in and effortless to manage. A place that’s resilient, guest-friendly, and designed for scale.
674|Set it up once, and it will save you hours in daily operations and protect your profit in the long run.

751|Take quality photos without hiring a pro
752|You don’t need to spend hundreds on a professional photographer to create a high-converting Airbnb listing. With the right approach, you can take stunning photos yourself, even with a smartphone. The key is understanding what guests want to see and how to showcase your space in the best possible light.
753|1. Clean and stage your space
754|Before taking any pictures, make sure everything is spotless and styled. Open the curtains, fluff the pillows, hide cords,... Remove bins that are visible, remotes,... It needs to showcase exactly how it is but you can definitely remove items for the picture if they make it look cluttered. Think of it like setting the stage; you want it to look welcoming, calm, and inviting.
755|2. Lighting is everything
756|Natural light is your best friend. Shoot during the day when your space is the brightest, ideally in the morning or late afternoon. Turn off overhead lights to avoid yellow tones. Open windows and let daylight in.
757|3. Use a good phone or camera
758|Most modern smartphones are more than enough. Just make sure your lens is clean and the resolution is set to high quality. Avoid using filters or heavy editing. Guests want to see the real space.
759|4. Shoot horizontally
760|Always take photos in landscape mode (horizontal), not vertical. Airbnb displays your listing photos in landscape, so this format gives a better view of each space.
761|5. Capture wide angles
762|Stand in corners or doorways to get as much of the room in the shot as possible. Use a wide-angle setting if available, but don’t distort the room. Guests want a real sense of space, not a fisheye view. The best setting is the 0.5 in Iphone pros.
763|6. Include every important area
764|Show everything: entryway, closets, appliances, amenities, parking, balcony, views. If it adds value, capture it. Lack of transparency leads to bad reviews.
765|7. Your photo order matters (this is crucial for bookings).
766|Use your most eye-catching image as cover photo. Bedrooms usually perform. Follow up with 4 key shots of different rooms that highlight your space’s best features. Airbnb shows only the first 5 photos before guests have to click “Show all photos,” so this is where you make a strong first impression and grab their attention.
