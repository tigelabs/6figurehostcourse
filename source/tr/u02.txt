244|Understanding local rules
245|<strong>Rules around Airbnb rentals vary widely depending on where you are. </strong>In general, larger cities tend to have stricter regulations due to housing shortages, tourism limits, and local government control.
246|Cities like Barcelona, Paris, New York City, and Amsterdam often require licenses, limit the number of rental nights, or restrict short-term rentals to certain zones.
247|<strong>That doesn't mean strictly regulated places are a no-go.</strong> We currently run five successful Airbnb apartments in Barcelona, one of the most difficult cities for short- term rentals. They all operate without a tourist license but are still legal because we focus on long-term stays of 31 nights or more, which are permitted under local law.
248|While long-term stays tend to generate less income per night than short-term bookings, they’re still highly profitable. Plus, they come with fewer guest turnovers, less day-to-day management, and more stability. You might not even need a manager or a full-time team.
249|On the other hand, <strong>some areas are much more flexible and easier to start in.</strong> Places like Bali, parts of Mexico, Portugal, and Southern Spain are known for being more open to short-term rentals, especially in tourist-friendly zones or when offering longer stays. This is why choosing the right location is key.
250|Before you list any property on Airbnb, it's crucial to understand the local laws and regulations in your area. Every city has its own rules when it comes to short-term rentals. Some require specific licenses or permits, others have restrictions on the number of nights you can rent, and some zones may not allow Airbnb at all.

337|Creative ways to make extra money
338|Being an Airbnb host isn’t just about charging for a night’s stay. You can boost your income by offering extra services and experiences; many of which can be set up passively. Here are some creative ideas:
339|<strong>Affiliate referrals:</strong> Some local websites offer referral (affiliate) programs for event tickets, tourist activities and restaurants, and pay you a commission when you send them customers. At one point, for example, we earned commission selling tickets for Barcelona parties to our guests. The commission was really high, around €10 per ticket, and in summer about 60% of our guests bought them, because it was an activity made for them.
340|<strong>Airport Transfers: </strong>Guests often prefer a smooth arrival, especially in unfamiliar destinations. Partner with a reliable local driver to offer airport pick-up. Add a commission for yourself or bundle it as a premium add-on.

379|Location <x1>vs</span> demand
380|what actually drives bookings
381|Most people think location is everything in Airbnb. And yes, being in a prime spot helps but it's not the full story. <strong>A great location with low demand will struggle. A less obvious location with high demand and the right setup? That can become a goldmine.</strong>
382|Location matters, but demand is what drives bookings.
383|Here’s how to think about it:
384|Location is fixed. It’s where your property is.
385|Demand is dynamic. It’s what people are searching for, when, and why.
386|The secret is to find the sweet spot where the two meet.
387|For example:
388|A beach town 20 minutes from a popular city might outperform a central apartment if it taps into weekend getaways and local tourism trends.
389|A quiet suburb near a hospital, university, or big event venue can have consistent bookings if you target the right audience (traveling nurses, researchers, digital nomads, etc.)
390|<strong>Don’t just chase the obvious tourist hotspots. </strong>Instead, ask:
391|Who is coming here?
392|Why are they coming?
393|When is demand highest?
394|What kind of stays are most common: short trips, weekend getaways, monthly relocations?
395|Use tools like AirDNA, AirROI or even just Airbnb’s own search filters to explore demand in specific areas. Look at occupancy rates, average nightly rates, and competition.
396|The goal isn’t just to be in a “good area.” The goal is to be in the right area for the right guest with a listing that speaks directly to what they need.
