1004|Hiring an agency or creating your own team
1005|We personally started by doing everything ourselves, not because we wanted to, but because we didn’t have the budget for a team in the beginning. And honestly, it was one of the best ways to learn the business inside out. But let’s be real cleaning toilets and fixing light bulbs isn’t what grows your business.
1006|While it’s helpful to understand how every part works, the manager role is by far the most important. That’s the person who keeps operations running, guests happy, and problems solved.
1007|Our recommendation: start small but strategic. Begin by hiring two cleaners (one main and one backup) and find a reliable freelance handyman who can handle basic maintenance.
1008|Then, for the first few weeks, manage the listing yourself. Respond to guests, handle check-ins, deal with minor issues, and file damage requests with Airbnb.
1009|This will teach you the platform, the guest journey, and the kinds of things that actually come up.
1010|Once that’s running smoothly and you know what the job involves, you’ll be in a much better position to hire a manager. You’ll understand how long tasks take, what tools they need, and what quality looks like.
1011|Ideally, your manager should live near the property so they can also serve as the emergency contact, someone who can be on-site quickly if there’s ever an issue while you’re away.
1012|This setup gives you the best of both worlds:
1013|You keep profit margins high
1014|You stay in control
1015|You gradually remove yourself from daily tasks without losing oversight
1016|Remember: you don’t need to work in your business. You want a business that works FOR you!
1017|We personally prefer this approach over using an agency because it allows us to build our own team with people we trust and vibe with, while still making the business fully passive over time.
1018|The best part? You stay in control. You set the systems, the standards, and the guest experience.
1019|With an agency, you’re tied to their team, their tools, and their way of working which may not match your vision or priorities. That’s why if you’re considering going the agency route, it’s crucial to find one that aligns with your values and expectations.
1020|It’s also worth running the numbers: compare what you’d spend paying your own team (cleaners, manager, handyman) vs. how much you’d lose by handing over 15–30% of your income to an agency.
1021|You might find that keeping things in-house or semi-in-house gives you more freedom and more profit without sacrificing quality.

1073|Building your own team
1074|When you’re launching your first Airbnb listing, you can start by doing things yourself but if you plan to scale, you'll want to build a small reliable team.
1075|Start with a cleaner.
1076|This is usually the first person you’ll need. Guests expect spotless spaces and tight turnover schedules. A good cleaner keeps your ratings high and prevents you from doing all the dirty work. Always hire a backup cleaner as well. Life happens, and you don’t want to be stuck when your main cleaner can’t make it.
1077|<strong>BONUS TIP:</strong> Instead of paying cleaners by the hour, we pay them per checkout. Every guest is different, some leave the place spotless, others leave a mess so hourly rates don’t always reflect the actual effort required. In our shared apartments, we use a hybrid model: a fixed rate for cleaning the common areas, plus an additional fee per room cleaned.
1078|One smart detail: the washing machine cycle takes about 1 hour and 15 minutes, so it naturally keeps the cleaner on-site for long enough to finish the full turnover properly.
1079|Next, find a handyman.
1080|A reliable handyman is essential for handling small repairs, maintenance issues, or unexpected fixes. You don’t need someone full-time, but it’s important to build a relationship with a trustworthy person you can call when something breaks.
1081|Once things are running smoothly, consider a manager or co-host.
1082|A manager handles the day-to-day operations like guest communication, check- ins, and coordinating the team. We recommend to hire someone who lives nearby so that they can also serve as your emergency contact and go to the property if needed. You can hire a cohost as your manager which you can find via the Airbnb platform itself.
1083|It’s smart to do this role yourself for the first few weeks to understand what’s involved. Then you’ll know exactly what to delegate and what expectations to set.
1084|How to find reliable people
1085|Hiring the right people can make or break your Airbnb business. A great team keeps everything running smoothly. The wrong people cost you time, money, and 5-star reviews.
1086|1. Ask other hosts for recommendations
1087|The best hires often come from word-of-mouth. Join local Facebook groups for Airbnb hosts or expats in your area and ask for trusted contacts.
1088|2. Start small with a test job
1089|Before giving someone full responsibility, assign a small task to test their reliability, communication, and attention to detail. For example: one trial cleaning or a basic repair.
1090|<strong>3. Be extremely clear with expectations &amp; allow access to your property wiki</strong> Remember: people can't read your mind. Share exactly what you expect, how you like things done, and when. The more detailed you are from day one, the better.
1091|Use templates, checklists and share the property wiki (see bonus template) to avoid miscommunication. Also have everything you agree upon in writing and make them sign it (eg: a contract or agreement, see bonus template on the next page).
1092|4. Pay on time and build a good relationship
1093|Reliable people stay when they’re treated well. Pay them fairly and consistently. Say thank you. Let them know they’re an important part of your team. A positive relationship leads to better work and longer partnerships.
1094|5. Have backups for every role
1095|Even the best people get sick, go on holiday, or move away. Always have at least one backup cleaner, handyman, and emergency contact. This gives you peace of mind and keeps your business running.
1096|Important to know: It’s completely normal to go through a few hires and fires before finding the right people that are reliable, trustworthy and that fit your standards. Don’t get discouraged, this is part of the process.

1132|Workflows for turnovers and check-ins
1133|Smooth turnovers and seamless check-ins are what separate amateur hosts from professional operators. If you want to scale or step away from daily management, you need systems that run without you.
1134|The more consistent your systems are, the less time you’ll spend managing small problems and the more scalable your business becomes. Turnovers and check-ins aren’t just chores they’re your frontline guest experience. Treat them like essential operations, and your reviews (and revenue) will show it.
1135|Here’s how to create workflows that are reliable, efficient, and easy for your team to follow:
1136|1. Create a turnover checklist &amp; efficient routine
1137|Hotels use a proven step-by-step routine for cleaning and for good reason. It creates consistency, saves time, and delivers a polished, professional result every time. You can steal the exact same strategy for your Airbnb!
1138|From how to enter the apartment to the order in which each room is cleaned, having a set process ensures that nothing gets missed and every turnover runs smoothly. It also makes it much easier to train new team members and maintain high standards across all your listings.
1139|You’ll find ready-to-use templates at the end of this chapter to help you set up and streamline your cleaning workflows.
1140|2. Use the same products and layout every time
1141|Keep your setup consistent. If the guest towels are always in the same drawer and the coffee is always on the left shelf, your team can work faster and guests won’t message you asking where things are.
1142|Stock the same items, in the same quantity, and store backup supplies in a labeled space (like a locked cabinet or storage box).
1143|3. rotate linens smartly
1144|Always have three sets of linens per bed and three sets of towels per guest:
1145|One in use
1146|One clean and ready
1147|One being washed
1148|This rotation ensures you’re never caught off guard on quick turnovers.
1149|4. create a space for lost &amp; found items
1150|Designate a small box or basket in a hidden but accessible spot (like a storage cabinet or supply closet) for any items guests leave behind. Instruct your cleaner to place forgotten belongings there during turnovers. Review and empty the box once a month, items can be discarded or given to the cleaner to take home. This simple system keeps your space tidy and avoids clutter from forgotten items.
1151|5. Have a backup plan
1152|Things happen. Cleaners get sick. Guests check in early. Internet goes down.
1153|Make sure you have:
1154|A backup cleaner or co-host you can call
1155|A process for emergency maintenance
1156|Clear instructions and SOPs that anyone can follow if you’re unavailable
1157|6. Train once, delegate forever
1158|Build a “property wiki”, a central place where everything about your listing lives: how to restock, where extra towels are, what to do if the toilet leaks. This makes onboarding new team members fast and easy, and reduces errors or questions.
1159|Remember: The most important tasks are mapped out for you, in order, inside your bonus “Airbnb roadmap: task checklist to launch your first listing.” <x1>Use it as your step-by-step guide</a> to stay on track and avoid missing anything important.
1160|Managing supplies and restocking
1161|Keeping your Airbnb running smoothly isn’t just about cleaning and check-ins. Behind the scenes, your ability to manage supplies and laundry efficiently can make or break the guest experience (and your sanity). The goal? No last-minute runs for toilet paper or stress over missing bed sheets.
1162|1. set up a clear inventory system
1163|Create a list of all essential supplies from toilet paper and soap to dish sponges, trash bags, and coffee. Track how much is used per week or per guest, and set a minimum stock level. When it hits that level, it’s time to restock.
1164|Pro tip: Use a Notion or Google Sheets tracker to log usage and quantities. You’ll find a template in the bonus resources.
1165|2. decide who’s responsible for restocking + working capital
1166|If you're delegating, decide who will buy the supplies (cleaner, manager, or you), how often, and how you’ll reimburse them. Use a small working capital, a set monthly amount kept in cash or digital wallet so your manager or cleaner doesn’t have to pay out of pocket. You can choose to top up the working capital either monthly or whenever the balance runs low. Just make sure there’s always enough available to avoid delays in restocking essentials.
1167|Tip: Keep all receipts organized in a shared folder or a Notion tracker to easily monitor expenses and stay on budget. For even more control, you can create a WhatsApp group where your team shares photos of receipts and updates the remaining balance after each purchase.

From how to clean the space, to restocking supplies, to handling late check-ins or maintenance issues, having it all documented means you don’t have to explain everything over and over again.
