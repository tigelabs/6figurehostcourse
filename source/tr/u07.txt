1244|The 3-step decluttering process for shared spaces
1245|Over time, shared spaces like the kitchen and bathroom can get cluttered with items left behind by previous guests or tenants, especially in longer stays or co-living setups. This creates mess, confusion, and even hygiene issues.
1246|To maintain a clean, welcoming space for everyone, it’s important to periodically reset and declutter. Here’s a simple system you can use to clear shared areas without causing misunderstandings or tension.
1247|There are two main approaches that we recommend to avoid removing items that are still in use.
1248|The “lost &amp; found” system
1249|Designate a specific spot for unclaimed or leftover items, like a basket in the bathroom or a drawer in the freezer. Let guests know that anything placed there will be removed after a set deadline.
1250|This method works especially well for things like shampoo bottles, freezer items, or personal belongings left in shared spaces.
1251|The label system
1252|Place a roll of tape and a marker near shared storage areas (like the fridge door or pantry). Ask guests to label their items with their name. Anything left unlabeled will be cleared out.
1253|Key areas to declutter:
1254|Shampoo and soap bottles in the shower
1255|Freezer and fridge door
1256|Unlabeled drawers and shelves
1257|Random items in common areas
1258|1. Start with a deep clean
1259|Start with a full cleaning of shared areas, especially the kitchen, bathroom, and hallway. This is the best time to spot forgotten items, expired food, or things taking up unnecessary space. Depending on the decluttering method you choose, either place these items in a designated removal spot or start labeling them with tape and a marker.
1260|2. Communicate clearly &amp; kindly
1261|Even if you've spoken to someone in person, follow up with a message to set clear expectations and avoid any surprises. Include a short deadline and explain the decluttering method to identify or collect their items. Make sure to send a reminder the day before you are removing the items.
1262|3. Set a clear removal date
1263|Give a 48–72 hour window for people to claim their belongings. After that, remove everything left behind. Optional: allow remaining items to be “adopted” before discarding eg: this is the last chance to collect your items. Anything still here after [time] is free to be taken or will be discarded.

1336|How to set up self-check-in
1337|(keys, locks, and codes)
1338|Self-check-in is one of the best systems you can implement as a host. It saves time, reduces stress, and allows your guests the flexibility to arrive whenever it suits them, even in the middle of the night. But to make it smooth and secure, you need the right setup.
1339|1. Choose your self-check-in method
1340|<strong>Smart locks</strong> (e.g., TTLock, Nuki, Igloohome): You can create digital codes for each guest and manage access remotely. Ideal for full automation. Make sure you buy one that also has the option to open with a key (for emergencies).
1341|<strong>Lockboxes</strong>: A physical box with a code that holds a key. Easy to set up, especially if your front door uses a traditional key.
1342|<strong>Smart door bell: </strong>If your apartment is in a building with an intercom you can install a smart device that connects to your intercom and automatically opens the building door when someone rings the bell.
1343|Tools like Nuki Opener, Bticino with Home+Control, or Ring Intercom (depending on your region) work well and are easy to install.
1344|Pro tip: Combine with a smart lock on your apartment door for fully remote check-ins, no key handovers needed.
1345|<strong>Key handoff at a nearby location:</strong> Less ideal, but still useful if digital access isn’t possible.
1346|Example: 'Keynest tool' allows you to drop off and collect keys at local partner locations (like supermarkets, key lockers or cafés). We use that for two of our apartments and it works very well. You only have to be careful for closing hours. Guests need to pick up the key before the place closes.
1347|Overview of all the keynest locations
1348|2. Where to place it
1349|If you're using a lockbox, make sure it's:
1350|Installed securely to a wall, gate, or railing near the entrance
1351|Well-lit at night
1352|Clearly mentioned in your check-in guide with photos
1353|If you're using a smart lock, make sure Wi-Fi or Bluetooth works well at the door, and test that codes work before the guest arrives.
1354|3. Set up your codes
1355|For smart locks, you can:
1356|Create a unique code per booking
1357|Set it to activate at check-in time and deactivate after checkout
1358|Reuse a master code for emergencies
1359|For lockboxes, change the code frequently (weekly or after each guest if possible) and avoid obvious codes like 0000 or 1234.
1360|4. Create a clear check-in guide
1361|Include step-by-step instructions
1362|Add photos of the lock, building entrance, and path to the door
1363|Mention Wi-Fi access, parking info, and anything they need to know before entering
1364|Send it the day before or morning of check-in
1365|IMPORTANT: Your instructions must be foolproof. Think of it this way: your guest is likely tired, possibly jet-lagged, standing outside a property they’ve never seen before, and struggling with low attention and patience.
1366|Your self-check-in instructions should be so clear that even a child could follow them.

1368|step instructions

1370|markers to show each part of the
1371|process (from the front gate to the
1372|exact location of the key box)
1373|Repeat the steps in written format in
1374|your message, even if you’re also
1375|using images or a PDF guide
1376|Never assume something is obvious. The more visual and detailed you make it, the fewer late-night calls you’ll get and the smoother your guest’s arrival will be.
