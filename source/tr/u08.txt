1384|If you choose to use <strong>physical keys,</strong> be aware that they often get lost. In places like Barcelona, guests sometimes lose them when their bags are stolen. Even when you ask guests to return the key to the original spot, many forget or ignore the instructions. Factor in this risk when deciding which system to use.
1385|Final note:
1386|Self-check-in is only stress-free when it works reliably. Test your system, update your instructions regularly, and always have a backup key or contact available in case something goes wrong.
1387|Which system fits your property?
1388|The right lock depends a lot on your region and on the type of property you have.
1389|<strong>Lockboxes don’t work everywhere.</strong> In Barcelona, for example, there is nowhere in the city to leave them. They often get broken, and boxes fixed to walls or railings end up cluttering the city.
1390|<strong>Some buildings don’t allow smart locks.</strong> Historic buildings are the typical case.
1391|So study your options before you buy. Every property can have self check-in: today there are alternatives on the market for almost every problem and situation. Try not to overcomplicate it.
1392|When checking in guests in person is the better choice
1393|There are specific cases where receiving the guest yourself, or having someone do it, makes sense. We have had properties with more tension with the neighbors: people who didn’t like having an Airbnb in the building.
1394|To avoid bothering them, we always had someone receive the guest. That way nobody was lost in the building or ringing the bell of the wrong apartment. Our person met the guest on arrival, called the elevator, showed the whole way, explained the apartment quickly, and the guest was settled and knew their way from then on.

1411|What to automate and what to delegate
1412|If you want to run a successful Airbnb with less stress and more freedom, the key is knowing the difference between what to automate, what to delegate, and what truly requires your attention.
1413|Automation saves you time, avoids human error, and keeps things running even when you're sleeping. If a task happens over and over again in the same way, it can probably be automated.
1414|Scheduled messages:
1415|Use scheduled messages in the Airbnb inbox to automate welcome messages, check-in info, checkout instructions, review requests, and FAQ answers.
1416|Important: Keep your communication simple and easy. Always think: could a 5-year old child understand this?
1417|People have low attention span when they're traveling, as they already have so many things to think about.
1418|Especially with self-check-in, only include the essential information they need to get in smoothly. If you want to share local tips or extra details, put them in a separate document or link, so the message stays clean and clear. You can also schedule a follow-up message for the day after arrival with helpful extras once they’ve settled in.
1419|Important: for essential information, make sure to enable the toggle that says “send for last-minute bookings and short stays” This ensures that last-minute bookings still receive the message, no matter when or for how long they book.
1420|Send your check-in photos inside the Airbnb chat
1421|Airbnb used to not allow photos in automated check-in messages, so you had to send them by hand. Today it does. Prepare your step-by-step check-in photos once, add them to your scheduled check-in message, and every guest receives the instructions with the pictures automatically. That is practically zero work for you.
1422|Keep the conversation on Airbnb, not on WhatsApp
1423|Don’t send guests to WhatsApp. It gives you more work, because you end up answering in two places. And only the conversations inside the Airbnb chat can be followed by Airbnb’s support team. If there is an incident or a conflict, or a guest claims you said one thing or another, messages outside Airbnb are not taken into account.
1424|The more you keep the conversation inside the Airbnb chat, the better.
1425|We only give a phone number for emergencies. Guests still sometimes write there to ask for the check-in instructions. When that happens we answer on WhatsApp with one line only, and then reply properly in the Airbnb chat:
1426|“This is an emergency number. I’ll reply to you in the Airbnb chat.”
1427|That sets the boundary. From then on, the guest talks to you on Airbnb.

1519|When to expand (and when to wait)
1520|One of the most exciting milestones in your Airbnb journey is the decision to expand. But growing too fast or too soon can backfire. Knowing when you're truly ready is key to long-term success.
1521|Are you ready to expand?
1522|You’re ready to expand if:
1523|<strong>Your current listing runs like a well-oiled machine:</strong> Systems are in place, guests are happy, reviews are great, and operations don’t depend on you 24/7.
1524|<strong>You’ve built a reliable team:</strong> Cleaners, a handyman, and a manager or co-host are already trained and capable of handling turnovers and guest issues without your direct involvement.
1525|<strong>You’ve automated repetitive tasks:</strong> Messaging, pricing, and check-ins run with minimal input thanks to tools like automated messaging, dynamic pricing tools, and smart locks.
1526|<strong>You consistently turn a profit:</strong> Your current property is generating reliable income, even during low season.
1527|<strong>You have capital (or cash flow) to invest:</strong> Expansion requires upfront costs. If you don’t have a buffer, the stress can outweigh the rewards.
1528|You should wait if:
1529|<strong>You’re still figuring out your systems:</strong> If your current property requires constant attention or if things often go wrong, focus on refining your process first.
1530|<strong>Your reviews are mixed or inconsistent:</strong> Expanding while you're still trying to deliver a great guest experience can compound problems and hurt your reputation.
1531|<strong>You don’t have a strong team in place:</strong> Trying to manage two properties without support will lead to burnout and mistakes.
1532|<strong>Cash flow is tight:</strong> Stretching your budget can put pressure on the guest experience, maintenance, and quality, which hurts long-term success.
1533|Expanding too soon is one of the most common mistakes new hosts make. Instead, focus on building a strong foundation, then scale intentionally. A second listing should feel like a natural next step, not a leap of faith. When in doubt, optimize before you expand.

1535|Managing multiple listings from different countries
1536|Expanding your Airbnb business across countries can be a powerful move. It allows you to diversify income, tap into different markets, and operate year-round without being tied to a single location. But it also comes with unique challenges especially when it comes to time zones, legal regulations, and team coordination.
1537|Here’s how to manage multiple listings across different countries efficiently:
1538|1. Build strong local teams
1539|Since you can’t be everywhere at once, your success depends on having a reliable on-the-ground team. This includes:
1540|Cleaners (with a backup)
1541|A local handyman
1542|A trusted property manager or co-host
1543|An emergency contact who lives nearby
1544|Invest time in finding, testing, and training your people. Once they’re solid, your properties can run with minimal input from you.
1545|2. Standardize everything
1546|Create templates, checklists, and systems that work across all properties:
1547|Cleaning checklists
1548|Guest message templates
1549|Supply restock trackers
1550|SOPs for turnovers and emergencies
1551|see bonus templates
1552|3. Use automation to your advantage
1553|Automated messaging keeps communication consistent.
1554|Dynamic pricing tools like PriceLabs or BeyondPricing help adapt to local demand.
1555|Smart locks and self check-in reduce the need for physical presence.
1556|Use time zone-based schedulers to avoid sending messages at 3am local time.
1557|4. Know the local laws and taxes
1558|Each country has its own rules for short-term rentals. Make sure you:
1559|Understand local licensing or registration requirements
1560|Know how taxes work (VAT, tourist tax, etc.)
1561|Hire local accountants or legal advisors if needed
1562|Compliance protects your business and avoids fines or shutdowns.
1563|5. Create financial separation
1564|Keep a separate bank account, payment method, and working capital for each property. This helps you track performance and simplifies tax reporting in each country.
1565|6. Centralize communication
1566|Use tools like WhatsApp groups or shared Google folders to stay in sync with your team. Make it clear who’s responsible for what, and set up simple routines (e.g., send a picture of every receipt or report checkouts daily).
1567|7. Protect your time and sanity
1568|Managing listings in different time zones can be overwhelming. Batch tasks by time zone and consider hiring a virtual assistant to help monitor messages while you sleep.
1569|S<em>tart with one country first and build solid systems. Once your first international listing runs smoothly without your constant involvement, you’ll have a blueprint to replicate elsewhere.</em>
1570|Managing properties in multiple countries is fully doable as long as you treat it like a business, not a hobby. Systems, teams, and tools are your best friends.
